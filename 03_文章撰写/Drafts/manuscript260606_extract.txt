[000] Cross-species OVAL glycan states reveal a matrix mechanism for avian shell-breaking mechanics
[001] OVAL glycans shape eggshell state
[002] [Please add full author names, affiliations, ORCID identifiers, equal-contribution notes, and corresponding-author details before submission]
[003] Abstract
[004] Eggshell matrix proteins are key regulators of eggshell structural formation, and existing studies have generated rich posttranslational-modification site maps, leaving the side-chain properties of glycosylation modifications as an important layer for further investigation. Using matched multi-layer analyses, we asked how glycan states on conserved matrix proteins map onto cross-species shell divergence. We compared chicken, duck, and pigeon under a conserved egg-tooth interface by integrating micro-CT morphometry, eggshell-matrix proteomics, intact glycopeptide mass spectrometry, Re-Glyco structural modelling, electrostatic analysis, and finite-element simulation. Cross-species separation emerged first in mammillary-layer organisation, while the matrix-protein toolkit remained largely shared. Within that shared background, ovalbumin (OVAL) shifted from High Mannose-dominant glycans in chicken to Neutral Complex/Hybrid-dominant glycans in duck and Sialylated Complex/Hybrid-dominant glycans in pigeon. These glycan states predicted a Ca²⁺surface-accessibility gradient on OVAL that provides a plausible route from glycan-modulated OVAL unfolding and matrix-bound nucleation-site exposure to mammillary-layer formation, mature mammillary density, and, after separating shell-thickness effects, local shell-breaking structural strength. Together, the data refine current understanding of avian eggshell formation by connecting Ca²⁺accessible matrix-protein surfaces to mammillary-layer organisation and to shell mechanics that can favour localized hatching.

[006] Introduction
[007] Avian eggshell formation is a rapid vertebrate mineralization process, assembling a mechanically competent calcitic shell within a narrow uterine time window. (1–3) This compressed schedule depends on an organic matrix-protein system that coordinates calcium delivery, nucleation, crystal growth, and shell architecture rather than acting as a passive scaffold. (1, 2, 4, 5) Bird hatching is a localized event: the egg-tooth presses on a small area of the inner shell rather than loading the whole shell at once. (6, 7) Comparable hatching-assist structures recur across egg-laying amniotes, so biologically meaningful variation in hatching performance is more likely to lie in the shell than in the tool itself. (6, 8–11) Yet current knowledge remains limited on how species with similar hatching mechanics produce distinct shell states across ecology and development. (12–16) The mammillary layer is the key entry point because it is where matrix-guided calcite nucleation first establishes the spacing and continuity inherited by later shell units, and because it is the first mechanically consequential shell layer encountered during inside-out hatching. The mechanistic question follows directly: once the shell-breaking interface is held constant, which molecular regulators at the mammillary layer account for the distinct eggshell states recovered across species? (1, 2, 4, 6, 17, 18)
[008] Eggshell matrix proteins regulate mammillary-layer mineralization, crystal growth, and mature shell architecture, and recurrent factors such as OC17, OC116, TRFE, and OVAL define a shared shell-building toolkit. (1, 2, 4, 5, 19–21) The unresolved issue is not toolkit presence, but how that toolkit is deployed across species. (1, 2, 4) This gap is sharpest for posttranslational modification. Phosphorylation and glycosylation sites are both catalogued, but phosphate side groups are comparatively similar whereas glycans vary widely in composition, size, and charge and can generate distinct molecular states on the same protein scaffold. (21–26) Glycan class is therefore a mechanistic variable, not just a feature of site occupancy. (24, 25) Glycoproteomic studies have shown that eggshell matrix proteins carry distinct N-glycosylation states, including glycosylated Asn in OC116 and defined OVAL-associated glycan compositions, but most analyses remained within one species, one compartment, or one site inventory at a time. (21, 23, 27–30) As a result, avian eggshell comparisons still rarely resolve matched glycan states on shared matrix proteins across species, leaving open whether that glycan layer explains why similar toolkits yield distinct shell states. (2, 4, 5, 23, 31)
[009] Glycosylation can alter protein stability, molecular recognition, and surface accessibility, and in other systems glycans act as dynamic shields that reshape accessible interfaces. (32–39) Layer-resolved eggshell work further showed that the same matrix protein can occupy different N-glycosylation states across shell compartments, implying that glycan state may redistribute function rather than merely decorate a fixed scaffold. (23) OVAL was therefore a tractable test case because it is abundant, mineralization-relevant, and linked to Ca²⁺responsive conformational behaviour during early shell formation. (2, 5, 23, 40) We anchored the comparison to the conserved egg-tooth interface and asked whether glycan-state differences on shared matrix proteins generate different Ca²⁺accessible surfaces at mineralization onset. (2, 6, 23)
[010] The missing step is a direct bridge from glycan class to surface presentation on a shared matrix background, and OVAL provided that bridge because its dominant glycan classes could be followed from glycoproteomics into structural modelling. (2, 5, 23, 32)
[011] Here, we compared chicken, duck, and pigeon. We established an integrated approach that combines micro-CT morphometry, comparative proteomics, intact glycopeptide mass spectrometry, Re-Glyco modelling with electrostatic analysis, and finite-element simulation for cross-scale analysis of eggshell structure, glycan state, and hatching mechanics. Our analyses reveal that mammillary-layer organisation separates the species before the broader matrix-protein toolkit does, and that OVAL glycan state is the strongest signal linking molecular state to Ca²⁺surface accessibility, mammillary-layer organisation, and hatching-favourable local mechanics. Together, these findings establish a framework for connecting glycan-state variation to eggshell formation and to the localized shell response used during inside-out hatching.
[012] Results
[013] The conserved hatching interface constrains shell variation
[014] We placed extant birds into a comparative space using 10,993 AVONET species records and selected three deliberately separated model species from that space (Fig. 1A). (6, 16, 41) This broader mapping prioritized two egg-relevant axes least likely to be secondary: nesting environment and offspring developmental state. Those axes span terrestrial-to-aquatic habitat use and a continuum from more precocial to more altricial young. (12, 41, 42) Within this comparison space, chicken, duck, and pigeon were therefore chosen near contrasting regions of those ecological-developmental gradients, reducing blurring by intermediate combinations. (16, 41–43) This functional grouping only partly overlaps with phylogeny. Chicken and duck remain closely related precocial taxa but separate along the habitat axis, whereas pigeon anchors the altricial end of the comparison (Fig. S2). (41–43) The design preserves shared ancestry while separating life histories within the same frame. The focal species differed in beak-tip geometry, but the egg-tooth remained a similarly localized dorsal breaker in all three species and therefore pointed to the same inside-out shell-breaking event during hatching (Fig. 1B). (6–8, 44) With that interface held constant, the next question is which eggshell layer first separates the species.
[015] Viewed through that shared hatching context, the first eggshell level to show a clear contrast in the present comparison was mammillary-layer morphology (Fig. 1C). In chicken, mammillae were smoother overall and formed rounded projections. In duck, mammillae showed more ridges and angular turns across the inner surface. pigeon was dominated by discrete triangular-conical mammillae. Three-dimensional surface reconstructions agreed with the cross-sectional views, indicating that the sampled inner-shell regions differed in mammillary geometry rather than representing minor variants of a shared inner-surface template.
[016] Quantification resolved the sampled regions in two related but not identical ways (Fig. 1D). Mammillary knob density was highest in chicken (171.36 ± 5.63 per mm2), exceeding both duck (155.22 ± 8.63 per mm2) and pigeon (158.27 ± 11.39 per mm2), while duck and pigeon remained similar to each other. By contrast, crystal-unit proportion was highest in pigeon (0.53 ± 0.04), intermediate in duck (0.44 ± 0.02), and lowest in chicken (0.40 ± 0.01). Within this scanned-fragment comparison, chicken showed the highest local mammillary density, whereas pigeon devoted the largest share of shell volume to crystal units grown from individual mammillary knobs. Duck remained intermediate in crystal-unit proportion while resembling pigeon in density. The two metrics did not collapse into one monotonic axis, but together they indicated that a mammillary-level contrast was already detectable before later shell traits were considered. Because this layer is the earliest structural level linked to eggshell mechanics and matrix control, we then asked a narrower question: did the observed contrast reflect wholesale toolkit replacement or differential use of a largely shared system? (1, 2, 17)

[021] Orthogroup analysis resolved the three eggshell matrix proteomes into a large shared core with smaller pairwise-shared and lineage-restricted complements (Fig. S3). At the overall level, the proteome still followed broad ancestry (Fig. S4), arguing against wholesale replacement of the eggshell-matrix toolkit.
[022] The shared core therefore became the relevant comparison frame. The next question was whether the species separated through wholesale proteome turnover or through different glycan states on shared matrix proteins.
[023] The chicken-exclusive set was simultaneously enriched for protein N-linked glycosylation (BP; Fig. S5), shifting the comparison from protein presence to chemical deployment. The retained shared core thus became the relevant molecular background. Glycosylation on shared proteins emerged as a proximate candidate layer for explaining divergence in mammillary organisation and downstream shell behaviour. (23) Most recurrent eggshell matrix proteins emphasized in earlier studies were recovered in the broader proteomic and glycoproteomic background here, indicating substantial agreement with prior eggshell-matrix work. The present dataset also broadened that comparative background. (1, 2, 4, 5, 19–21)
[024] OVAL glycosylation gives the clearest cross-species molecular contrast
[025] Intact-glycopeptide profiling showed that the three species differed in sampling depth but still shared a stable comparison core (Fig. 2A to D). The cluster view recovered 25 clusters shared by all three species, with the largest additional pairwise overlap between duck and pigeon at 64 clusters, whereas chicken contributed little species-private cluster space (Fig. 2A).The same pattern held for the catalog counts: duck yielded 321 glycoproteins, 547 glycosites, and 197 glycan compositions; pigeon yielded 192, 257, and 162; and chicken yielded 55, 88, and 105 (Fig. 2B). Shared-core Jensen-Shannon similarity remained between 0.33 and 0.40, with the duck-pigeon pair highest (Fig. 2C). These values indicate divergence within a still comparable glycoproteomic background rather than three disconnected chemical spaces.Glycan-class composition reinforced the same point at the chemical-deployment level. High-Mannose and Complex-Fucosylated glycans formed a broad cross-species background, whereas Complex-Sialylated and other more extended classes contributed more strongly to lineage separation (Fig. 2D).

[028] A stricter BlastP-based filter retained an orthologous glycoprotein subset suitable for structural comparison and summarized that shared candidate space in Fig. 2E. Using chicken as the reference, non-reference candidates were retained only when the mean E-value was below 1 × 10-5 and sequence identity met the final comparability thresholds. This restricted the downstream comparison to high-confidence orthologs. Under that stricter mapping, OC17 was glycosylated only in chicken, whereas OC116, TRFE, and OVAL all retained glycosylation signals across the three species and served as shared anchors. Among them, OVAL showed a clear cross-species glycan shift and was prioritised for structural analysis.
[029] Integrated protein and glycan abundance profiles further identified OVAL as the shared protein most closely aligned with the cross-species eggshell differences. In chicken, the protein-abundance and glycan-abundance space showed weak overall coupling, and OVAL occupied a comparatively modest glycan-burden position among the highlighted matrix proteins (Fig. 3A). In duck, the same analysis showed stronger positive protein-glycan coupling, with OVAL retaining high protein abundance while shifting toward higher glycan output (Fig. 3B). In pigeon, protein-glycan coupling was again positive, and OVAL carried the strongest glycan burden among the three species while remaining within the shared matrix-protein background (Fig. 3C). Pairwise enrichment plots then showed why OVAL remained the most interpretable discriminator. In the chicken-versus-pigeon plane, OVAL shifted toward the glycan-rich side of the comparison, indicating that its glycan change exceeded the corresponding protein-abundance change (Fig. 3D). In the chicken-versus-duck plane, OVAL again deviated from the equal-change diagonal, separating chicken from duck through glycan enrichment rather than simple protein abundance (Fig. 3E). In the duck-versus-pigeon plane, OVAL remained displaced from protein-glycan equivalence, preserving a glycan-state ordering beyond the chicken-centered comparisons (Fig. 3F). Among the highlighted eggshell-matrix proteins, OVAL therefore remained abundant in all three species but differed sharply in glycan burden: relatively modest in chicken, stronger in duck, and strongest in pigeon. OC116 and TRFE remained informative shared proteins, but neither separated bulk protein abundance from glycan output as consistently as OVAL. Intact-glycopeptide assignments placed OVAL along a coherent cross-species progression. Chicken carried compact High-Mannose glycans, duck was enriched for Neutral Complex/Hybrid glycans, and pigeon carried more extended Sialylated Complex/Hybrid glycans. Taken together, the six Fig. 3 panels place OVAL among the shared proteins whose glycosylation aligns most closely with the phenotype ordering recovered here.
[030] Because those OVAL glycan classes differ strongly in size and charge distribution, the more informative comparative variable was OVAL surface accessibility rather than OVAL abundance alone. The relevant feature was how much of the acidic OVAL interface remained chemically reachable once decorated by different glycans. Ortholog control, abundance decoupling, and glycan-class progression together left OVAL as the shared candidate that was most directly comparable, chemically specific, and structurally actionable.

[033] OVAL glycan state reshapes surface accessibility
[034] OVAL was selected for structural analysis because it remained shared, chemically distinct, and directly comparable across species. Dominant glycosylated OVAL ensembles and matched apo references were rebuilt to test whether the three species differed mainly through glycan-dependent surface behavior rather than through backbone sequence alone. (2, 40) Representative rebuilt glycan conformations and species-specific surface maps showed that the dominant glycans occupied different spatial envelopes on the same folded protein scaffold (Fig. 4A and B). In panel A, points 1 and 2 mark the glycan end-to-end vector, point 3 marks the glycan centroid, point 4 marks the protein Cα centroid, and the translucent sphere shows the radius-of-gyration envelope centered on point 3. Panel B then translates that geometry into surface exposure by coloring Ca²⁺relevant regions and blackening the same regions after shielding.
[035] Pigeon first separated by occupying the largest overall glycan envelope, as shown by the higher radius of gyration in Fig. 4C and the longer end-to-end distances in Fig. 4E. In panel A terms, that means a longer 1-2 span and a larger sphere around point 3, with point 4 serving as the protein anchor that the glycan is measured against. That expansion occurred together with closer local approaches to the backbone in Fig. 4D and a broader glycan-protein distance distribution in Fig. 4F, consistent with extended glycans that sample a larger envelope while still folding back toward the OVAL surface. Chicken defined the opposite endpoint, with compact glycans and the weakest geometric intrusion into the acidic interface. Duck remained closer to chicken in radius of gyration and overall glycan-protein spacing, but it separated from both species in end-to-end span and minimum backbone approach. Fig. 4C to F therefore translate glycan-class progression into a shielding geometry: a smaller sphere and shorter 1-2 span in panel A indicate a more compact glycan state, whereas a larger sphere and longer 1-2 span indicate a more extended, surface-engaging state.
[036] Fig. 4G to J resolved the same acidic interface at progressively stricter levels. Viewed against panel B, the colored patches are the Ca²⁺relevant regions that remain accessible, and black marks the same regions after shielding. Fig. 4G therefore measures gross interface shielding, Fig. 4H asks what fraction of candidate acidic residues remained hotspots, Fig. 4I measures the surface area retained by hotspot residues, and Fig. 4J counts the subset of Ca²⁺hotspots that remained both electrostatically favorable and physically reachable. Interface shielding increased stepwise from chicken to duck to pigeon, and the same ordering was retained across hotspot surface area, hotspot fraction, and net accessible Ca²⁺hotspots. Together, these panels show progressive masking of the shared acidic OVAL face during early mineralization.
[037] Matched glycosylated-versus-apo comparisons then showed that glycan addition changed the number of Ca²⁺relevant hotspot residues and the exposed carboxylate surface most clearly in pigeon (Fig. 4K and L; Fig. S10). With panel B in mind, glycosylation preserves or hides the same Ca²⁺relevant patches rather than creating new ones: the colored patches remain reachable, whereas the black patches are the same sites after shielding. Duck shifted in the same direction without a resolved structure-level significance call, and chicken could be assessed only descriptively because one glycosylated structure was available. This pattern is more consistent with a glycan-imposed shift in the acidic surface presented at mineralization onset than with a generic sequence effect alone. Fig. 4K to N then collapse the same comparison to the whole-interface level. Across those panels, chicken preserved the most accessible Ca²⁺relevant surface, pigeon shifted the largest share into a glycan-affected state, and duck trended toward the lower-accessibility side but did not separate from pigeon or apo references uniformly across metrics. Chicken therefore retained the highest inferred Ca²⁺capturing capacity and the state most compatible with earlier Ca²⁺responsive opening of OVAL at mineralization onset. Duck and pigeon moved toward the lower-accessibility side from different structural backgrounds. The same ordering matched the phenotype sequence: chicken combined the densest mammillary field with the strongest local response to inside-out loading, whereas duck and pigeon converged toward the lower-response side. Taken together, Fig. 4A to N link glycan-dependent separation, glycan geometry, interface masking, and Ca²⁺relevant accessibility on a shared matrix protein. (2, 5, 40) This ordering places OVAL glycan-dependent Ca²⁺accessibility upstream of glycan-modulated OVAL unfolding and nucleation-site exposure, providing the structural premise for testing whether the resulting mammillary organisation affects local shell-breaking mechanics.

[042] Inside-out loading resolves hatching-relevant local mechanics
[043] Finite-element testing translated the shared egg-tooth interface into an explicit inside-out loading design. Fig. 5A shows the species-specific dorsal beak views and the matched micro-CT-derived finite-element shell fragments used for loading. Because the meshes preserved species-specific shell geometry, the analysis remained anchored to the same mammillary context identified morphologically. Impact loading was sampled across multiple offset positions on the eggshell fragments, yielding independent contact-force and contact-shear-stress time courses for each species (Fig. 5B and C). We recorded both raw peak contact force (F_max) and peak contact shear stress (τ_max) so that thickness-driven force effects could be separated from the local stress response at the mammillary contact interface. Peak τ_max was used as the direct readout of hatching-relevant local shell response. Species means ± s.d. were calculated across the sampled positions (Fig. S11A to F; eggshell thicknesses: chicken 0.29 mm, duck 0.35 mm, and pigeon 0.19 mm). (6, 44, 45)
[046] Fig. 5. Local finite-element loading connects egg-tooth contact geometry to hatching-relevant shell mechanics. (A) Dorsal beak views and matched micro-CT shell-fragment finite-element models for chicken, pigeon, and duck from top to bottom. Each model shows the conical egg-tooth impactor and a representative local stress field on the reconstructed shell geometry. (B) Mean contact-force time courses across nine impact positions, with shaded ±1σ envelopes, and the corresponding peak contact force (F_max) distribution. Star markers indicate the mean peak value on each time-course trace. (C) Mean contact shear-stress time courses across the same nine positions, with shaded ±1σ envelopes, and the corresponding peak shear stress (τ_max) distribution. Star markers indicate the mean peak value on each trace. Box-plot points denote individual impact positions (n = 9 per species), bars show mean ± s.d., p values above the plots are one-way ANOVA omnibus p values, and different letters indicate Tukey HSD groupings at p < 0.05. Simulations used identical loading and material settings so that the comparisons isolate geometry-dependent local response.
[048] Peak F_max differed significantly among species (p = 1.64 × 10^-13). chicken reached 1.12 ± 0.11 N, duck reached 0.90 ± 0.09 N, and pigeon reached 0.49 ± 0.04 N, and all pairwise differences were significant by Tukey HSD (Fig. 5B). By contrast, τ_max resolved a two-level pattern (p = 6.64 × 10^-10). chicken reached 551.60 ± 108.80 MPa and was significantly higher than duck at 404.00 ± 39.60 MPa and pigeon at 393.00 ± 35.20 MPa. The latter two species did not differ significantly from each other (p = 0.728; Fig. 5C).
[049] The difference between F_max and τ_max clarified the duck result. Its higher raw contact force was driven mainly by greater shell thickness (0.35 mm versus 0.19 mm in pigeon). It did not indicate superior unit-area material resistance. By contrast, chicken exhibited a 36-40% increase in τ_max relative to the two other species, indicating a stronger hatching-relevant local stress response independent of shell thickness. This high-versus-low grouping, with chicken alone in the high group and duck together with pigeon in the low group, matched the grouping recovered for mammillary density by Tukey HSD (Fig. 1D). The mechanics therefore retained the contrast already recovered from mammillary organisation and OVAL accessibility.
[050] Whole-shell fracture force alone could make duck appear mechanically superior to chicken because of its greater shell thickness, despite the absence of the same high-density mammillary state. By focusing instead on the local stress response at the micro-CT-derived mammillary interface, τ_max removes that ambiguity. It shows that the high-density chicken state remains distinct, whereas duck and pigeon converge at a lower local-response level. This functional readout preserved the same asymmetry already visible in earlier sections and linked glycosylation-associated differences to local shell-breaking mechanics across the three model species.
[051] Discussion
[052] Cross-species divergence in this dataset resolved first at the mammillary layer, not at the level of wholesale matrix-protein turnover. Within a largely shared eggshell-matrix toolkit, OVAL glycan state provided the clearest molecular axis linking Ca²⁺relevant surface accessibility, mammillary-layer organisation, and hatching-relevant local mechanics. (1, 6, 23)
[053] The three-species design matters because eggshell traits are organized along continuous ecological and developmental gradients rather than by one binary label. (12, 14) Nesting environment tracks a terrestrial-to-aquatic axis, and offspring condition spans a continuum from more precocial to more altricial young. Neither dimension is well represented by a simple yes-or-no partition. (42, 43) Duck is particularly informative in that design. It retains a broadly precocial developmental condition and the greatest shell thickness in this comparison, yet it shifts toward an intermediate OVAL glycan state and accessibility profile, and its τ_max outcome converges with pigeon rather than with chicken. Duck therefore separates shell-thickness buffering from the Ca²⁺accessibility-mammillary-mechanics axis emphasized here. The comparison samples deliberately separated regions of a continuous ecological-developmental space while keeping the hatching interface itself comparable. (12, 41–43)
[054] Mammillary-layer mineralization mode remains the central structural level in the interpretation. (1, 17) In this model, glycan-dependent exposure of acidic OVAL surfaces would influence Ca²⁺access and OVAL unfolding during the earliest matrix-mineral interaction, thereby changing nucleation-site presentation, mammillary-layer formation, mature mammillary density, and local hatching mechanics. Once early calcite crystal units are established, later eggshell regions inherit the spacing logic set in that first mineralization window. A dense mammillary field can therefore alter matrix retention, mineral continuity, local stress redistribution, and mature morphology. (1, 46, 47) That emphasis is consistent with earlier eggshell studies that place the mammillary layer at the intersection of crystal nucleation and matrix control. The present comparison extends that line by linking the layer to a cross-species glycan-state readout rather than to shell-quality descriptors alone. (1, 17, 48, 49) Recent poultry omics studies increasingly tie age, shell-gland transcription, extracellular-vesicle cargo, and other whole-shell quality traits to eggshell phenotype. Those descriptors, however, usually remain broader than the proximate material layer isolated here. (18, 50–52) For that reason, mammillary organisation is not merely another shell trait, but the earliest material context in which matrix chemistry can plausibly bias later mechanical outcome. (1, 4) That structural position is why mammillary organisation is the first cross-species difference that can be read as potentially consequential rather than merely descriptive. (1, 17)
[055] Among the molecular layers examined here, OVAL N-glycan architecture most closely tracked the structural contrast recovered across species. Orthogroup turnover, gene-family change, and glycoprotein-network divergence still matter, but they mainly define comparative background rather than the nearest explanation. OVAL glycan state is especially informative because it is shared across species, chemically interpretable, and situated on an abundant matrix protein already implicated in mineralization. (2, 23, 53) Earlier work had already kept OVAL in view as an abundant eggshell glycoprotein and mineralization candidate. Prior glycoproteomic studies also showed that eggshell-matrix proteins can occupy different N-glycosylation states. The advance here is therefore not simply the detection of more glycopeptides; it is the ortholog-resolved comparison of which glycan states align most consistently with phenotype and how those assignments carry into structural and mechanical interpretation. (2, 23) Earlier chicken studies established a glycosite foundation for OVAL and identified glycosylated Asn in OC116. The present dataset resolved dominant glycan classes on the corresponding OVAL ortholog sequons carried into structural modelling (chicken N293; duck and pigeon N97). Relative to earlier site-detection studies, this comparison extends breadth across species and into glycan-class interpretation rather than merely lengthening a within-species site inventory. (21, 23, 28) OVAL is useful not because it is unique, but because it remains comparable across species while retaining interpretable chemical divergence at the level of specific glycan classes. (21, 23, 28)
[056] The non-OVAL signals still matter. OC116 and TRFE remained informative shared proteins, whereas OC17 appeared glycosylated only in chicken and may therefore represent a more lineage-restricted mineralization program. (5, 19, 20) Earlier eggshell studies had already assigned functional importance to OC17, OC116, and ovotransferrin-related matrix components, and the present dataset does not overturn that view. Instead, these proteins define the biological backdrop more consistently than they define the main cross-species discriminator recovered here. (5, 19, 20) For OC116 in particular, earlier biochemical work had already established glycosylated Asn in chicken eggshell matrix. More recent avian palaeoproteomic work showed that OC116 is among the more sequence-variable avian eggshell proteins. It shows substantial intraspecies variability rather than acting as a uniformly stable species marker. That broader context fits our comparison: retained glycosylation keeps OC116 biologically relevant, but it does not recover as stable a structure-linked contrast as OVAL. (21, 31) The same point also clarifies how the present study relates to earlier eggshell glycoproteomics. Those studies were essential for showing that eggshell matrix proteins can be glycosylated and that site-level inventories are experimentally accessible. Our contribution is the next comparative step: shared orthologs, dominant glycan classes, and the consequence of those classes for surface presentation across species. (21, 23) The shared toolkit therefore remains multicomponent, even though OVAL provides the most experimentally tractable entry point.
[057] Re-Glyco and APBS analyses provide the structural bridge in the argument. Across species, this produced a glycan-state gradient: compact chicken glycans preserved the most accessible acidic surface, neutral complex/hybrid duck glycans imposed an intermediate constraint, and extended sialylated pigeon glycans generated the strongest steric and electrostatic shielding. Earlier in vitro and structural work had already suggested that OVAL conformation and electrostatics matter during mineralization, but matched glycoform-resolved surface ensembles had not been compared across bird species. (2, 40) Glycan-state variation is therefore resolved here as a physically interpretable surface difference. Although this result does not establish direct causality, it supports a restrained inference: different glycan states on the same matrix protein can alter the chemical surface presented to the mineralizing environment and may thereby contribute to the structural divergence observed here.
[058] The mechanical comparison targets inside-out loading during hatching rather than conventional outside compression or whole-shell fracture. That choice is critical: eggshell thickness inflates absolute failure load, whereas τ_max is less thickness-confounded and more directly reports stress transfer through the mammillary interface. (6, 45, 54) Sun et al. likewise showed from longitudinal and latitudinal measurements that eggshell thickness varies across the whole egg, with the circumferential zone around the blunt end being locally thinnest, reinforcing the need to separate global shell-thickness buffering from local hatching-interface mechanics. (55) The analysis therefore asks whether the inner mammillary interface retains the same contrast already inferred from matrix chemistry and morphology. (6, 45, 54, 56) Duck makes this separation explicit: its thicker shell increased F_max but did not recreate the high-τ_max state observed in chicken. This distinction separates thickness buffering and developmental background from the material pathway emphasized here. Eggshell thickness, body size, broad reproductive ecology, and lineage history all contribute background structure. (43, 57) Yet thickness-only explanations do not account for τ_max, and diffuse lineage-divergence explanations do not explain why the same ordering recurs across glycan class, electrostatic accessibility, mammillary organisation, and hatching-relevant mechanics. (6, 44) What recurs is the same alignment: glycan state, Ca²⁺surface accessibility, mammillary organisation, and τ_max under inside-out loading. Ecology and phylogeny define the design space, whereas matrix-protein glycan state remains the nearest chemically interpretable layer recovered here. (2, 23) Duck and pigeon remain essential because they bound the hatching-relevant mechanical axis from different directions. Duck combines greater shell thickness with intermediate OVAL accessibility and low τ_max, showing that thickness alone does not recreate the chicken local-response state. Pigeon converges with duck at low τ_max from a thinner shell, more extended OVAL glycans, and a different mammillary background. Together, these contrasts make chicken a useful reference for linking glycan-dependent matrix behaviour to an eggshell state that concentrates local mechanical response at the hatching interface. In this framework, OVAL glycan state is the most directly readable molecular layer through which that state becomes mechanically interpretable.
[059] A second interpretive issue is that the mammillary layer can be partly resorbed during late incubation and hatching. That possibility does not erase the relevance of the present comparison because the quantified descriptors used here were mammillary density and crystal-unit organisation. Those features remain embedded in the shell even when part of the innermost material has been absorbed. The same consideration guided the mechanical readout. We emphasized the second characteristic peak rather than the first because the earliest force excursion is dominated more strongly by initial morphology-dependent contact, whereas the later peak more faithfully reflects stress transmission through the shell wall as a whole.
[060] Taken together, the comparison converged on a hatching-favourable local eggshell state in this dataset. Chicken combined the densest mammillary field, the least shielded Ca²⁺relevant OVAL surface, and the strongest local stress response under inside-out loading. Duck showed why the chain cannot be reduced to shell thickness: despite its thicker shell and higher F_max than pigeon, its τ_max grouped with pigeon rather than chicken. This pattern supports the inference that chemically specific states on reused matrix proteins can organise mineralised phenotypes more directly than proteome turnover alone in this comparison. (58–60)
[061] The same analytical sequence may extend beyond avian eggshells. Many biomineralization systems depend on organic matrices that regulate ion access, surface exposure, and mineral nucleation through chemically specific interfacial states rather than bulk composition alone. In that broader frame, the present workflow offers a way to move from glycoproteomic state to surface presentation and then to mesoscale function. Similar scale-bridging problems arise in other mineralized tissues and biomimetic materials. The same logic may also inform regenerative settings in which eggshell-derived materials or membrane proteins are being explored for tissue engineering and bone repair. More broadly, the study offers a template for testing how chemically specific matrix states organize biomineral behavior across biological and biomedical contexts. (58, 59, 61, 62)
[062] The present scope remains bounded. We analyzed dominant glycoforms rather than the full in vivo glycan ensemble. We also treated each species as mechanically uniform at the scale of the mean eggshell and relied on incompletely constrained uterine ionic conditions in the APBS framework. A practical limitation for immediate wet-lab follow-up is that, with current toolkits, implementing both species-matched expression of orthologous matrix proteins and precise installation of predefined glycan structures at specific glycosylation sites within a single framework can remain nontrivial in practice. In parallel, biomineralization operates as a large coupled reaction system, which can limit straightforward single-variable interpretation from any one assay. The next decisive tests are therefore defined-glycoform mineralization assays, direct manipulation of OVAL glycosylation in chicken, and site-resolved validation of the same inside-out mechanical contrast, together with methodological development to improve causal resolution. These experiments should clarify whether the OVAL glycan state directly participates in shell mineralization or instead marks the high-mammillary-density eggshell state with unusual fidelity.
[063] In summary, this study links mammillary organisation, glycoprotein state, Ca²⁺surface accessibility, and local hatching mechanics across three avian eggshells. Chicken defined the high-mammillary-density end of that axis, with dense mammillary organisation, compact OVAL glycans, greater Ca²⁺relevant surface exposure, and the strongest local response at the mammillary interface. Duck occupied the critical intermediate position: its thicker shell increased absolute force but did not reproduce the same local stress state, separating shell thickness from the mammillary-interface mechanism. As comparable glycoform assignments become available, the same framework can extend to other abundant eggshell matrix proteins. Across morphometric, glycoproteomic, structural, and mechanical layers, OVAL glycan state remains the most consistently aligned molecular feature of the high-mammillary-density eggshell state recovered here.
[064] Materials and Methods
[065] Biological materials
[066] Fertilized eggs were collected during the mid-laying period from three avian lines: seven eggs from Chahua pink-shelled laying hens, seven eggs from Shaoxing spotted green-shelled laying ducks, and 19 eggs from White King egg pigeons. Gallus gallus eggs were obtained from the Poultry Resources Conservation Farm, China Agricultural University (Beijing, China); Columba livia eggs were provided by Prof. Chang Yu, College of Veterinary Medicine, China Agricultural University; and Anas platyrhynchos eggs were supplied by Jinxing Duck Industry (Beijing, China). All eggs were stored at 16°C for 7 d under breeder-egg holding conditions before analysis.
[067] Eggshell matrix protein extraction
[068] Eggshell matrix proteins were extracted from the eggshell mammillary layer (EML) by an established EDTA demineralization protocol. Eggs were rinsed with deionized water and placed in sterile sealed bags. One egg from each species was reserved for micro-CT analysis; the remaining shells were used for matrix-protein extraction. The remaining six chicken eggs and six duck eggs were divided into three two-egg extraction units per species, whereas the remaining 18 pigeon eggs were divided into three six-egg extraction units. These pooled extraction units were used as the matched biological samples for both shotgun proteomics and intact glycopeptide analysis. For G. gallus and A. platyrhynchos, the eggshell cuticle layer (ECL) was removed prior to EML extraction by treatment with 15 mL of 5% EDTA (0.13 mol/L, pH 7.6) supplemented with 2-mercaptoethanol (10 mmol/L) for 30 min at 20°C, with gentle manual kneading to separate the ECL; eggshells were subsequently rinsed with deionized water. No discrete cuticle layer was recognized in pigeon eggshells, so C. livia shells were rinsed with distilled water and entered the same extraction workflow directly. EML proteins from all three species were then solubilized under the same EDTA–2-mercaptoethanol conditions with the extraction duration extended to 12 h at 20°C. The resulting suspension was centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 15 min; the pellet was resuspended and centrifuged a second time, and the pooled supernatant was stored at −80°C until analysis. Species were processed in parallel under the same extraction chemistry so that downstream differences were less likely to reflect handling drift.
[069] Micro-CT imaging and mammillary morphometry
[070] One eggshell fragment (4 mm × 4 mm) from each species was excised from the equatorial region near the blunt-half midpoint and scanned using a Phoenix V|tome|x M microfocus CT system (GE Sensing and Inspection Technologies GmbH, Wunstorf, Germany). The X-ray source was operated at 85 kV tube voltage and 160 μA current without a beam filter; scan settings were held constant across all specimens, and a total of 1,800 projection images were collected for each eggshell. Reconstructed image volumes were exported as 16-bit unsigned isotropic datasets with a sampling distance of approximately 0.003836 mm along the x, y, and z axes (about 3.84 μm voxel size). After scanning, each fragment was subdivided evenly into nine subfragments for downstream regional quantification. Volumetric data were reconstructed in 3D Slicer, and the eggshell region was segmented with the Threshold module in the Segmentation workflow using operator-guided threshold correction after automatic selection. The same operator reviewed all three species side by side; grayscale thresholds were fine-tuned to retain all true shell voxels while preserving visible pore openings. Acquisition noise was suppressed by a 5 × 5 × 5 median filter, followed by largest-island isolation and 9 × 9 × 9 hole-filling. Within each subfragment, three morphometric parameters were then calculated from the labelmap. The segmented shell model was first duplicated as a single-copy volume and solid-filled with the Fill Holes operation; subtraction of the original shell model from the filled solid yielded the mammillary interspace layer, and closed voids appearing in that interspace plane were defined as mammillary knobs. Mammillary density was calculated as mammilla count divided by analysis-unit area. Total eggshell volume in the same analysis unit was obtained directly from the labelmap, and mean column-unit volume was defined as total shell volume divided by mammilla count; column-unit volume fraction was then calculated as mean column-unit volume divided by the total eggshell volume of the corresponding analysis unit. Because the columnar units initiated by mammillae are arranged as repetitive and approximately even planar units in normal avian eggshell microstructure, these parameters were treated as local average representatives of whole-shell organisation. The nine observations per species were non-overlapping regional subsamples from one scanned fragment and were used to quantify within-fragment spatial variation around that local mean rather than to represent nine independent eggs. The same segmentation and post-processing workflow was applied to all scans so that species contrasts reflected morphology rather than reconstruction settings. (1, 46)
[071] Shotgun proteomics of eggshell matrix proteins
[072] For shotgun proteomics, eggshell-matrix extracts from the post-CT sample set were analyzed as three pooled biological samples per species, with each chicken and duck sample comprising two eggs and each pigeon sample comprising six eggs. Proteins were extracted by resuspension in lysis buffer (1% SDS, 1% protease inhibitor cocktail), sonication on ice, and clarification by centrifugation at 12,000 × g at 4°C for 10 min; protein concentration was determined with a BCA assay kit. Proteins were precipitated with pre-cooled acetone (5 volumes, −20°C, 2 h), washed twice with acetone, and redissolved in 200 mM TEAB. Disulfide bonds were reduced with 5 mM dithiothreitol (56°C, 30 min) and alkylated with 11 mM iodoacetamide (room temperature, 15 min, dark). They were digested overnight with sequencing-grade trypsin (enzyme:protein ratio 1:50) and desalted with Strata X SPE columns. No offline HPLC fractionation was performed before LC-MS/MS analysis. For proteome acquisition, 20 μg peptide was injected per run.
[073] Desalted peptides were dissolved in mobile phase A (0.1% formic acid in water) and separated on a home-made 15-cm × 100-μm i.d. reversed-phase C18 analytical column connected to a Vanquish Neo UPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Mobile phase B consisted of 0.1% formic acid in 80% acetonitrile, and the flow rate was maintained at 400 nl/min. The gradient program was 4% B for 0–0.5 min, 4–8% B for 0.5–0.6 min, 8–22.5% B for 0.6–13.6 min, 22.5–35% B for 13.6–20.5 min, 35–55% B for 20.5–20.9 min, 55–99% B for 20.9–21.4 min, and 99% B for 21.4–22.6 min. After UPLC separation, peptides were introduced into a nano-electrospray ionization source operated at 1,900 V and analyzed on an Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Full-MS spectra were acquired in the Orbitrap at 240,000 resolution over 380–980 m/z; MS/MS fragments were acquired in the Astral analyzer at 80,000 resolution using HCD fragmentation in DIA mode (NCE = 25%), fixed first mass 150 m/z, AGC target 500%, and maximum injection time 3 ms. DIA data were processed with DIA-NN v1.8 against species-specific reference proteomes — G. gallus (43,711 entries), A. platyrhynchos (91,801 entries), and C. livia (17,309 entries), all downloaded August 2024 — concatenated with reverse decoy sequences. Using species-matched reference databases reduced the risk that peptide assignments would be biased toward the best-annotated proteome. Trypsin/P cleavage was specified with up to one missed cleavage; N-terminal methionine excision and carbamidomethylation of Cys were set as fixed modifications. Protein and peptide FDR were each controlled at < 1%.
[074] Intact glycopeptide mass spectrometry
[075] For intact glycopeptide analysis, the same three pooled biological samples per species were carried forward, and 200 μg peptide digest from each pooled sample was used as input for enrichment. N-glycopeptides were enriched from tryptic digests by hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography (HILIC). Peptide digests were redissolved in loading buffer (80% ACN, 5% TFA), loaded onto a HILIC column, washed three times with loading buffer, and glycopeptides were eluted twice with 0.1% TFA, 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate, and 50% ACN. Eluates were desalted with C18 Zip-Tips and vacuum-dried. Glycopeptide fractions were separated on the same nano-LC platform using a 34-min gradient (4–99% B at 400 nl/min). Full-MS spectra were acquired at 240,000 resolution over 700–2,000 m/z; MS/MS spectra were acquired at 80,000 resolution with fixed first mass 120 m/z, cycle time 0.6 s, AGC target 100%, intensity threshold 25,000 ions/s, and maximum injection time 5 ms. Raw DDA data were processed with MSFragger v3.4 against the same species-specific reference proteomes as above, with strict trypsin cleavage (up to 2 missed cleavages), peptide length 7–50 residues, fixed carbamidomethylation of Cys, variable N-terminal acetylation and Met oxidation, and default MSFragger glycosylation mass offsets. Protein, peptide, and PSM FDR were each controlled at < 1%. N-glycan structural classes were assigned using the Oxford nomenclature into six categories: High-Mannose, Paucimannose/Truncated, Neutral Complex/Hybrid, Fucosylated Complex/Hybrid, Sialylated Complex/Hybrid, and Other. Per-protein per-species class abundance was computed as the fraction of total glycan-site signal intensity. Structural classes were assigned after site-level identification so that shared glycan usage could be compared on a common protein background across species.
[076] Comparative proteome analysis and gene-family evolution
[077] Protein sequences from all three species were assigned to orthogroups using OrthoFinder (all-versus-all BlastP; E-value threshold 1 × 10⁻¹⁰; MCL inflation parameter 2.0), yielding a 3,250-orthogroup background set for all downstream enrichment analyses. Divergence times were taken from published timetrees: G. gallus–A. platyrhynchos 83.37 Mya and G. gallus–C. livia 90.84 Mya. Gene-family expansion and contraction rates were inferred with CAFE5 using the time-calibrated phylogeny. GO enrichment of pairwise-shared, species-exclusive, expanding, and contracting orthogroup sets was performed using the OrthoVenn3 web platform (https://orthovenn3.bioinfotoolkits.net) against the 3,250-orthogroup background; terms with adjusted p < 0.05 were considered significant. (43, 57, 63)
[078] Cross-species glycoprotein ortholog identification
[079] High-confidence cross-species orthologues of four target eggshell glycoproteins (OVAL, OC116, TRFE, OC17) were identified by BlastP of each reference G. gallus sequence against the non-reference-species proteomes (E-value threshold 1 × 10⁻⁵; maximum 500 target sequences; 250 reported alignments). Candidate hits were retained at average maximum sequence identity ≥ 0.80; where query and subject non-overlapping HSP counts were unequal, a relaxed threshold of ≥ 0.50 was applied. Final UniProt ortholog identifiers used for downstream structural and quantitative analyses are listed in Supplementary Table 1.
[080] Integrated protein-glycan abundance comparison
[081] For Fig. 3A to C, protein-level and glycan-site quantification tables were loaded separately for each species from the Protein_quant and Site_quant sheets. When the Number Comparable field was present, protein entries and glycan-site entries with values < 2 were excluded so that only features detected in at least two of the three pooled biological samples were retained for downstream comparable-protein analysis. Mean protein intensity was calculated across all species-matched intensity columns for each accession, and mean glycan intensity was calculated for each quantified glycosylation site across the corresponding site-intensity columns. Only positive-intensity entries were retained. Glycan-site rows were then inner-joined to protein rows by protein accession so that each point represented one quantified glycosylated sequon with a matched protein-abundance measurement. Protein and glycan intensities were log2-transformed, and within-species protein-glycan coupling was summarized by Spearman rank correlation with two-sided p values. OVAL, OC116, TRFE, and OC17 were highlighted by the strict ortholog assignments summarized in Fig. 2E, and labels were annotated with the corresponding glycosylated Asn position.
[082] For Fig. 3D to F, pairwise glycan-protein enrichment plots were built from ortholog-mapped protein and glycan abundance differences between species. Protein abundance for each accession was defined as the mean of nonzero replicate intensities after excluding proteins with Number Comparable < 2 when available. Glycan abundance was defined at the protein level as the sum of mean nonzero site intensities across all quantified glycosylation sites assigned to that accession after the same comparable-feature filtering. Gallus-versus-Anas and Gallus-versus-Columba comparison spaces were built from blastp outfmt 6 mappings, retaining the best hit per query when the mean E value was <= 1 × 10-5 and the average sequence identity was >= 0.40; when query and subject had different numbers of non-overlapping HSPs, the maximum identity threshold >= 0.40 was applied instead. The Anas-versus-Columba plane was bridged through shared Gallus orthologs that passed the same filter in both datasets. For each retained ortholog pair, the x coordinate was calculated as log2(I_ref) - log2(I_comp) and the y coordinate as log2(G_ref) - log2(G_comp), where I and G denote protein and glycan abundance, respectively. The y = x diagonal therefore marked matched protein-glycan change, whereas displacement toward the glycan-rich side identified proteins whose glycan shift exceeded the corresponding change in bulk protein abundance.
[083] N-glycan structural ensemble modelling
[084] OVAL ortholog protein structures (AlphaFold2-predicted models) were accessed through the GlycoShape platform (glycoshape.org) via UniProt accession identifiers. Experimentally detected N-glycan compositions from IGP-MS were mapped to the GlycoShape glycan library by monoisotopic mass matching (tolerance ±0.5 Da), using per-residue masses of 203.0794 Da (HexNAc), 162.0528 Da (Hex), 291.0954 Da (NeuAc), 146.0579 Da (dHex), and 132.0423 Da (Pen) with an 18.0106 Da water correction; matched entries were retrieved as GlyTouCan accession identifiers. All experimentally detected glycoforms that could be matched to the GlycoShape library were retained for downstream modelling rather than pre-filtering to a dominant subset. Full conformational ensembles were then generated with the GlycoShape Re-Glyco Ensemble tool (glycoshape.org/ensemble), which restores missing glycans by aligning them to torsion angles from Privateer crystallographic standards and sampling conformations from the GlycoShape molecular-dynamics ensemble library. For each OVAL ortholog, glycan modelling was constrained to the experimentally detected glycopeptide site identified in the present dataset; each matched glycan was then submitted as an independent modelling job via the GlycoShape API and attached to the measured target sequon — G. gallus N293 and A. platyrhynchos / C. livia N97 — with ensemble size 50, random seed 42, and PDB output format. This procedure yielded 50 glycoprotein models for G. gallus (1 matched glycan type), 150 for A. platyrhynchos (3 types), and 700 for C. livia (14 types, including four NeuAc positional isomers resolved from the GlycoShape library). Using the same random seed and ensemble size for every submitted glycan maintained matched sampling depth across species. Ensemble geometric descriptors were calculated for each model from atomic coordinates using BioPython: the radius of gyration (Rg) of all glycan heavy atoms, the end-to-end distance of the glycan chain, and the minimum distance between any glycan heavy atom and protein Cα atoms (minimum Cα contact distance). Per-structure summary statistics (mean ± s.d.) and pairwise species comparisons (Mann–Whitney U test, two-tailed) were performed for each descriptor. (40, 64, 65)
[085] Electrostatic potential calculation
[086] Electrostatic surface potentials were computed for each Re-Glyco ensemble model and a matched apo reference with glycans removed using APBS v3.4.1. Atomic partial charges and radii were assigned with PDB2PQR using the CHARMM36 force field and PROPKA protonation at pH 7.4; glycan heavy-atom partial charges were assigned from published GLYCAM06 values. APBS input grids were generated automatically from the PQR bounding box with 10 Å padding and a target grid spacing of 0.5 Å; focusing grids were set to 70% of the coarse-grid lengths. The nonlinear Poisson–Boltzmann equation was solved with single-ion boundary conditions, 0.15 mol/L monovalent salt (ion radii 2.0 and 1.8 Å for cation and anion, respectively), protein dielectric 2.0, solvent dielectric 78.54, solvent-accessible surface definition smol, charge discretization spl0, solvent probe radius 1.4 Å, spline window 0.3 Å, surface density 10.0, and temperature 298.15 K. Solvent-accessible surface areas were calculated by the Shrake–Rupley algorithm; surface residues were defined by relative ASA ≥ 0.25. Ca²⁺-binding electrostatic hotspots were defined as surface Asp or Glu residues with APBS potential < −5 kT/e. Ensemble-level metrics reported include hotspot count (N_hot), per-hotspot mean SASA, hotspot fraction of total surface Asp/Glu, and median surface electrostatic potential. The same electrostatic threshold and surface-definition rules were applied to all models to preserve cross-species comparability. (32, 33, 66)
[087] Finite-element analysis
[088] The region of interest used for downstream finite-element analysis was defined as a cylindrical volume of 1 mm radius during micro-CT reconstruction. Surface models derived from micro-CT were first exported as STL files and reverse-engineered in Geomagic Wrap for finite-element pre-processing by sequential de-noising (strength 2), triangle simplification to approximately 300,000 faces, mesh re-gridding at 0.01 mm, iterative defect correction to zero residual faults, and organic parametric surface fitting at minimum tolerance. The resulting eggshell surface models were then imported into Ansys Workbench 2023 R1 and solved with the explicit LS-DYNA module (unit system: mm/kg/N/s). To isolate structure-driven mechanical differences, identical eggshell material properties were assigned across species; parameter values were adopted from the avian eggshell elasticity study reported in Biology 10, 989 (2021; DOI: 10.3390/biology10100989) rather than re-estimated separately for each species. In the solver keyword deck, the eggshell was modeled with *MAT_PLASTIC_KINEMATIC and *SECTION_SOLID, using density 2770 kg/m^3, Young's modulus 3.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa, Poisson's ratio 0.33, yield strength 1.5 × 10⁷ Pa, tangent modulus 0, and maximum equivalent plastic strain at failure 0.05. This explicit impact setup followed the general logic of crash-deformation simulations, but was rescaled to the local eggshell loading geometry studied here. The impactor, used to simulate the egg tooth, was a frustum (base radius 0.1 mm; top radius 0.5 mm; height 0.5 mm) assigned the library IRON-ARMCO explicit material and meshed as a separate solid part. Contact between the impactor and eggshell was defined with *CONTACT_AUTOMATIC_SURFACE_TO_SURFACE using a friction coefficient of 0.2. Eggshell mesh element sizes were 0.05 mm (G. gallus), 0.05 mm (A. platyrhynchos), and 0.03 mm (C. livia), ensuring at least six element layers across the eggshell cross-section; the impactor was meshed at 0.1 mm. The impactor was driven by an imposed initial velocity of 50,000 mm/s along the loading axis, whereas one boundary set on the fragment perimeter was fully fixed in all translational and rotational degrees of freedom. Analyses ran for 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ s with a time-step safety factor of 0.7, erosion enabled, a minimum time step of 1 × 10⁻⁸ s, and automatic mass scaling; solver outputs included GLSTAT, SPCFORC, RCFORC, NCFORC, BNDOUT, NODOUT, MATSUM, ELOUT, JNTFORC, and DEFORC at 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ s intervals, with D3PLOT and INTFOR written every 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ s. For the positional loading analysis, the impactor was sampled at nine lateral offsets arranged on a 3 × 3 grid with 0.5 mm spacing. Across these nine cases, only the impactor coordinates were translated; material definitions, contact settings, boundary conditions, fragment size, and all other solver controls were held constant. Peak contact force (F_max) and peak contact shear stress (τ_max) were extracted for each position. Sampling nine offsets allowed local positional heterogeneity to be measured without changing fragment size or loading geometry between species. (6, 44, 67)
[089] Statistical analysis
[090] All values are expressed as mean ± s.d. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant throughout. Normality was evaluated by the Shapiro–Wilk test and homogeneity of variance by Levene's test before parametric between-species analyses. Mammillary morphometric parameters were compared among species by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's honestly significant difference test (Tukey HSD; α = 0.05). These nine morphometric observations per species were non-overlapping subfragments from one scanned fragment and should therefore be interpreted as within-fragment regional replicates rather than as nine independent biological samples. The same assumption checks supported one-way ANOVA with Tukey HSD for finite-element outcomes (F_max and τ_max). In contrast, glycan ensemble geometric descriptors (Rg, end-to-end distance, minimum glycan–protein contact distance) and hotspot-derived ensemble metrics did not satisfy normality and/or homoscedasticity across species, so pairwise species contrasts for these variables were evaluated with two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests. Glycosylation-induced reduction in N_hot within C. livia was assessed by one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test versus the apo reference value; total Asp/Glu SASA at the whole-interface level was summarized descriptively because the structure-level values were invariant across C. livia glycoforms; shifts in median surface electrostatic potential were assessed by one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test against the apo reference value. No multiple-testing correction was applied, and no outliers were removed. All statistical analyses were conducted in Python using scipy.stats and statsmodels.

[003] 抽象的
[004] 蛋壳基质蛋白是蛋壳结构形成的关键调控因子，已有研究已经建立了丰富的翻译后修饰位点图谱，而糖基化修饰侧链性质仍是有待进一步解析的重要层面。 使用匹配的多层分析，我们询问保守的 基质蛋白 上的 糖链状态 如何映射到跨物种壳分歧。 我们通过集成 显微CT 形态测定、蛋壳 基质蛋白质组学、完整的 糖肽 质谱、Re-Glyco 结构建模、静电分析和有限元模拟。 跨物种分离首先出现在 乳突层 组织中，而 基质蛋白 工具包在很大程度上仍然是共享的。 在该共同背景下，卵清蛋白（OVAL）从鸡中的高甘露糖主导型糖链转变为鸭中的中性复合物/混合主导型糖链和鸽中的唾液酸化复合物/混合主导型糖链。 这些 糖链状态 预测了 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 上的 Ca²⁺ 表面可及性梯度，提供了从 糖链调控的OVAL解折叠 和 基质结合成核位点暴露 到 乳突层形成、成熟乳突层密度 的合理路线，以及在分离壳厚度效应后的 局部破壳结构强度。 这些数据通过将 Ca²⁺可及的基质蛋白表面 与 乳突层 组织和有利于局部孵化的 蛋壳力学 连接起来，完善了当前对鸟类 蛋壳 形成的理解。
[005] 预告片：OVAL糖链状态 揭示了 蛋壳 基质化学如何塑造孵化过程中使用的壳区域。
[006] 介绍
[007] 禽类 蛋壳 结构是 快速的脊椎动物矿化过程，在狭窄的子宫时间窗口内组装具有机械能力的方解石壳。 (1-3) 这种压缩的时间表取决于协调 钙供应、成核、晶体生长和壳结构的 有机基质蛋白系统，而不是充当被动支架。 (1,2,4,5) 鸟孵化是一个局部事件：卵齿 挤压内壳的一小部分区域，而不是立即加载整个壳。 (6, 7) 类似的孵化辅助结构在产卵羊膜动物中重复出现，因此孵化性能的具有生物学意义的变化更有可能存在于壳中而不是工具本身。 (6, 8–11) 然而，对于具有相似孵化机制的物种如何在生态和发育过程中产生不同的壳状态，目前的知识仍然有限。 (12–16) 乳突层 是关键入口点，因为它是 基质引导的方解石成核 首先建立由 后续蛋壳结构单元 继承的 间距和连续性 的地方，并且因为它是 由内向外 孵化过程中遇到的第一个机械结果壳层。 机械问题直接随之而来：一旦破壳界面保持恒定，乳突层 上的哪些分子调节器可以解释跨物种恢复的不同 蛋壳 状态？ （1、2、4、6、17、18）
[008] 蛋壳 基质蛋白s 调节 乳突层 矿化、晶体生长和成熟的壳结构，而 OC17、OC116、TRFE 和 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 等经常性因子定义了共享的壳构建工具包。 (1, 2, 4, 5, 19–21) 未解决的问题不是工具包的存在，而是该工具包如何跨物种部署。 (1,2,4) 这种差距对于翻译后修饰来说最为明显。 磷酸化和 糖基化 位点均已编录，但磷酸侧基相对相似，而 糖链 在组成、大小和电荷方面差异很大，并且可以在同一蛋白质支架上产生不同的分子状态。 (21-26) 因此，糖链 等级是一个机械变量，而不仅仅是场地占用的一个特征。 (24, 25) 糖蛋白质组学研究表明，蛋壳 基质蛋白 具有不同的 N-糖基化 状态，包括 OC116 中的糖基化 Asn 和定义的 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 相关 糖链 组合物，但大多数分析一次仍停留在一个物种、一个区室或一个位点库存内。 (21, 23, 27–30) 因此，鸟类 蛋壳 比较仍然很少解析跨物种共享 基质蛋白 上的匹配 糖链状态，而 糖链 层是否解释了为什么相似的工具包会产生不同的外壳状态仍然悬而未决。 （2、4、5、23、31）
[009] 糖基化 可以改变蛋白质稳定性、分子识别和表面可访问性，而在其他系统中，糖链 充当动态屏蔽，重塑可访问界面。 (32–39) 层解析的 蛋壳 工作进一步表明，相同的 基质蛋白 可以跨壳隔室占据不同的 N-糖基化 状态，这意味着 糖链状态 可以重新分配功能，而不仅仅是装饰固定的支架。 (23) 因此，卵清蛋白（OVAL） 是一个易于处理的测试用例，因为它丰富、与矿化相关，并且与早期壳形成过程中的 Ca²⁺响应性 构象行为相关。 (2, 5, 23, 40) 我们将比较与保守的 卵齿 界面进行了比较，并询问共享 基质蛋白 上的 糖链状态 差异是否会在矿化开始时生成不同的 Ca²⁺可及性ible 表面。 （2、6、23）
[010] 缺少的步骤是从 糖链 类到共享矩阵背景上的表面呈现的直接桥梁，而 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 提供了该桥梁，因为它的主要 糖链 类可以从糖蛋白组学跟踪到结构建模。 （2、5、23、32）
[011] 在这里，我们比较了 鸡、鸭 和 鸽。 我们建立了一种综合方法，结合了 显微CT 形态测定、比较蛋白质组学、完整的 糖肽 质谱、Re-Glyco 建模与静电分析以及 有限元 模拟，用于 蛋壳 结构、糖链状态 和孵化力学的跨尺度分析。 我们的分析表明，乳突层 组织先于更广泛的 基质蛋白 工具包分离物种，并且 OVAL糖链状态 是将分子状态与 Ca²⁺ 表面可及性、乳突层 组织和 有利于破壳 局部力学联系起来的最强信号。 总之，这些发现建立了一个框架，用于将 糖链状态 变异与 蛋壳 形成以及 由内向外 孵化期间使用的局部壳响应联系起来。
[012] 结果
[013] 保守的孵化界面限制了壳的变异
[014] 我们使用 10,993 个 AVONET 物种记录将现存鸟类放入一个比较空间，并从该空间中故意选择了三个分离的模式物种（图 1A）。 (6, 16, 41) 这种更广泛的映射优先考虑了两个最不可能成为次要的与卵相关的轴：筑巢环境和后代发育状态。 这些轴跨越了陆地到水生栖息地的利用，以及从早熟到晚熟幼体的连续体。 (12, 41, 42) 在这个比较空间中，鸡、鸭 和 鸽 因此被选择在这些生态发展梯度的对比区域附近，从而减少中间组合的模糊。 (16, 41–43) 这种功能分组与系统发育仅部分重叠。 鸡和鸭仍然与早成类群密切相关，但沿栖息地轴分开，而鸽则锚定了比较的晚熟端（图S2）。 (41-43) 该设计保留了共同的祖先，同时将同一框架内的生活史分开。 焦点物种的喙尖几何形状不同，但 卵齿 在所有三个物种中仍然是类似的局部背破壳，因此指向孵化期间相同的 由内向外 破壳事件（图 1B）。 (6–8, 44) 在该界面保持不变的情况下，下一个问题是 蛋壳 的哪个层首先分离物种。
[015] 通过共享的孵化环境来看，在本次比较中第一个显示出明显对比的 蛋壳 水平是 乳突层 形态（图 1C）。 在鸡中，乳头整体上更光滑，并形成圆形突起。 在 鸭 中，乳头在内表面上显示出更多的脊和角转。 鸽 以离散的三角圆锥形乳头为主。 三维表面重建与横截面视图一致，表明采样的内壳区域在 乳突层 几何形状上有所不同，而不是代表共享内表面模板的微小变体。
[016] 量化以两种相关但不相同的方式解析采样区域（图 1D）。 乳突层旋钮密度在鸡中最高（171.36±5.63/mm2），超过鸭（155.22±8.63/mm2）和鸽（158.27±11.39/mm2），而鸭和鸽 彼此保持相似。 相比之下，鸽的晶体单元比例最高（0.53±0.04），鸭居中（0.44±0.02），鸡最低（0.40±0.01）。 在这次扫描片段比较中，鸡 显示了最高的局部 乳突层 密度，而 鸽 将最大份额的壳体积投入到从单个 乳突层 旋钮生长的晶体单元中。 鸭 的晶体单元比例保持中等，而密度与 鸽 相似。 这两个指标并未折叠成一个单调轴，但它们一起表明，在考虑后来的贝壳特征之前，乳突层 级别的对比度已经可以检测到。 因为这一层是与 Egg蛋壳力学 和矩阵控制相关的最早的结构级别，所以我们提出了一个更狭窄的问题：观察到的对比是否反映了大规模工具包替换或很大程度上共享系统的差异使用？ (1,2,17)

[021] 正交群分析将三个 蛋壳 基质蛋白质组解析为一个大的共享核心，具有较小的成对共享和谱系限制的互补体（图 S3）。 在整体水平上，蛋白质组仍然遵循广泛的祖先（图S4），反对大规模替换蛋壳-matrix工具包。
[022] 因此，共享核心成为相关的比较框架。 下一个问题是物种是通过批发蛋白质组周转分离还是通过共享 基质蛋白 上的不同 糖链状态 分离。
[023] 鸡 专有组同时富集了蛋白质 N 连接的 糖基化（BP；图 S5），将比较从蛋白质存在转移到化学部署。 保留的共享核心因此成为相关的分子背景。 共享蛋白质上的 糖基化 成为解释 乳突层 组织和下游外壳行为差异的最接近候选层。 (23) 早期研究中强调的大多数重复出现的 蛋壳 基质蛋白 在更广泛的蛋白质组和糖蛋白质组背景中得到恢复，表明与先前的 蛋壳 矩阵工作基本一致。 目前的数据集也拓宽了这种比较背景。 （1、2、4、5、19-21）
[024] 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 糖基化 给出最清晰的跨物种分子对比
[025] Intact-糖肽 分析表明，这三个物种的采样深度不同，但仍然具有稳定的比较核心（图 2A 至 D）。 簇视图恢复了所有三个物种共享的 25 个簇，鸭 和 鸽 之间最大的额外成对重叠为 64 个簇，而 鸡 贡献了很少的物种私有簇空间（图 2A）。目录计数也保持相同的模式：鸭 产生 321 个 糖蛋白、547 个糖位点，和197个糖链组合物； 鸽 产生 192、257 和 162； 鸡 得到 55、88 和 105（图 2B）。 共享核心 Jensen-Shannon 相似度保持在 0.33 到 0.40 之间，其中 鸭-鸽 对最高（图 2C）。 这些值表明在仍然可比的糖蛋白组背景中存在分歧，而不是三个不连续的化学空间。糖链 级组合物在化学部署水平上强化了同一点。 高甘露糖和复合岩藻糖基化 糖链 形成了广泛的跨物种背景，而复合唾液酸化和其他更扩展的类别对谱系分离的贡献更大（图 2D）。
[027] 图 2. 共享核心糖蛋白组架构和 糖链 级跨物种部署。 (A) 糖蛋白质组数据集中按物种划分的簇计数。 (B) 每个物种中检测到的 糖蛋白、糖位点和 糖链 组合物的数量。 (C) 物种间共享核心 Jensen-Shannon 相似性。 (D) 糖链 类的物种级分布（高甘露糖、寡甘露糖、杂交、复杂-普通、复杂-岩藻糖基化、复杂-唾液酸化等）。 (E) Ortholog-糖链 和弦图，将 蛋壳 糖蛋白 链接到 鸡、鸭 和 鸽 中的主要 糖链 类，并保留突出显示的 基质蛋白 以供下游分析。
[028] 更严格的基于 BlastP 的过滤器保留了适合结构比较的同源 糖蛋白 子集，并总结了图 2E 中的共享候选空间。 使用 鸡 作为参考，仅当平均 E 值低于 1 × 10-5 并且序列同一性满足最终可比性阈值时才保留非参考候选。 这限制了下游与高置信度直向同源物的比较。 在更严格的映射下，OC17仅在鸡中被糖基化，而OC116、TRFE和卵清蛋白（OVAL）在三个物种中都保留了糖基化信号并充当共享锚。 其中，卵清蛋白（OVAL）表现出明显的跨物种糖链转变，优先进行结构分析。
[029] 整合的蛋白质和 糖链 丰度谱进一步确定 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 是与跨物种 蛋壳 差异最接近的共享蛋白质。 在鸡中，蛋白质丰度和糖链丰度空间显示出弱的整体耦合，并且卵清蛋白（OVAL）在突出显示的基质蛋白中占据相对适中的糖链负担位置（图3A）。 在鸭中，相同的分析显示更强的正蛋白N-糖链耦合，卵清蛋白（OVAL）保留高蛋白质丰度，同时转向更高的糖链输出（图3B）。 在鸽中，proteiN-糖链耦合再次呈阳性，卵清蛋白（OVAL）在三个物种中携带最强的糖链负担，同时保持在共享的基质蛋白背景内（图3C）。 成对富集图随后显示了为什么 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 仍然是最可解释的鉴别器。 在鸡与鸽平面中，卵清蛋白（OVAL）向比较中富含糖链的一侧移动，表明其糖链的变化超过了相应的蛋白质丰度变化（图3D）。 在鸡与鸭平面中，卵清蛋白（OVAL）再次偏离等变化对角线，通过糖链富集而不是简单的蛋白质丰度将鸡与鸭分开（图3E）。 在鸭与鸽平面中，卵清蛋白（OVAL）仍然从proteiN-糖链等价物中移位，保留了超出以鸡为中心的比较的糖链状态排序（图3F）。 因此，在突出显示的 蛋壳-基质蛋白 中，卵清蛋白（OVAL） 在所有三个物种中均保持丰富，但在 糖链 负担中存在显着差异：鸡 相对温和，鸭 较强，鸽 最强。 OC116 和 TRFE 仍然具有信息共享的蛋白质，但都没有像 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 那样从 糖链 输出中分离大量蛋白质丰度。 完整的 糖肽 分配使 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 沿着连贯的跨物种进展。 鸡携带紧凑的高甘露糖糖链，鸭针对中性复合物/混合糖链进行富集，而鸽携带更多扩展的唾液酸化复合物/混合糖链。 总而言之，图 3 的六个图将 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 置于共享蛋白质之中，其 糖基化 与此处恢复的表型排序最接近。
[030] 由于这些 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 糖链 类别在尺寸和电荷分布方面差异很大，因此更具信息性的比较变量是 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 表面可及性，而不是单独的 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 丰度。 相关特征是，一旦被不同的 糖链 修饰后，有多少酸性 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 界面仍然保持化学可达性。 直系同源控制、丰度解耦和 糖链 级进展共同使 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 成为最直接可比、化学特异性和结构可操作的共享候选者。
[032] 图 3. 直向同源限制丰度-糖链 分析将 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 确定为主要的共享鉴别器。 (A 到 C) log2 转换蛋白质丰度与 鸡、鸭 和 鸽 中 糖链 丰度的蛋白质型协同进化图。 插图报告 Spearman 的 rho 和两侧 p 值。 突出显示的标签表示保留的 基质蛋白（卵清蛋白（OVAL）、OC116、TRFE 和 OC17）。 (D 到 F) 鸡 与 鸽、鸡 与 鸭 以及 鸭 与 鸽 的成对二维 糖链 蛋白质富集图。 虚线对角线表示相同的蛋白质和 糖链 变化。
[033] OVAL糖链状态重塑表面可达性
[034] 选择 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 进行结构分析是因为它在不同物种之间保持共享、化学上不同且可直接比较。 重建显性糖基化 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 整体和匹配的 apo 参考，以测试这三个物种是否主要通过 糖链 依赖的表面行为而不是仅通过主链序列进行差异。 (2, 40) 代表性重建的 糖链 构象和物种特异性表面图显示，占主导地位的 糖链 在同一折叠蛋白质支架上占据不同的空间包络（图 4A 和 B）。 在面板 A 中，点 1 和 2 标记 糖链 端到端向量，点 3 标记 糖链 质心，点 4 标记蛋白质 Cα 质心，半透明球体显示以点 3 为中心的回转半径包络线。 然后，面板 B 通过对 Ca²⁺相关 区域进行着色并在屏蔽后将相同区域变黑，将该几何形状转换为表面暴露。
[035] 鸽 首先通过占据最大的整体 糖链 包络线进行分离，如图 4C 中较高的 回转半径 和图 4E 中较长的端到端距离所示。 在 A 组术语中，这意味着点 3 周围有更长的 1-2 跨度和更大的球体，点 4 充当 糖链 测量的蛋白质锚点。 这种扩展与图 4D 中更接近主干的局部方法和图 4F 中更广泛的 糖链-蛋白质距离分布一起发生，与扩展的 糖链 一致，该扩展的 糖链 采样更大的包膜，同时仍然向 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 表面折叠。 鸡 定义了相反的端点，具有紧凑的 糖链 和对酸性界面的最弱的几何侵入。 鸭 在 回转半径 和整体 糖链 蛋白质间距上仍然更接近 鸡，但它在端到端跨度和最小主链方法上与两个物种分开。 因此，图 4C 至 F 将 糖链 级级数转化为屏蔽几何形状：图 A 中较小的球体和较短的 1-2 跨度表示更紧凑的 糖链状态，而较大的球体和较长的 1-2 跨度表示更扩展的表面接合状态。
[036] 图 4G 至 J 以逐渐严格的水平解析了相同的酸性界面。 从面板 B 上看，彩色补丁是仍可访问的 Ca²⁺相关 区域，黑色标记屏蔽后的相同区域。 因此，图 4G 测量了总体界面屏蔽，图 4H 询问了 热点 中剩余的候选酸性残基的比例，图 4I 测量了 热点 残基保留的表面积，图 4J 计算了 Ca²⁺热点 中保持静电有利且物理可达的子集。 接口屏蔽从 鸡 逐步增加到 鸭 再到 鸽，并且在 热点 表面积、热点 分数和净可访问 Ca²⁺热点 上保留相同的顺序。 这些面板共同显示了早期矿化过程中共享酸性 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 面的逐渐掩盖。
[037] 然后匹配的糖基化与apo比较表明，糖链的添加改变了Ca²⁺相关 热点残基的数量，并且在鸽中最清楚地改变了暴露的羧酸盐表面（图4K和L；图S10）。 考虑到面板 B，糖基化 保留或隐藏相同的 Ca²⁺相关 补丁，而不是创建新补丁：彩色补丁保持可达，而黑色补丁在屏蔽后是相同的位置。 鸭 在没有解析结构水平显着性调用的情况下朝相同方向移动，并且 鸡 只能进行描述性评估，因为存在一种糖基化结构。 与单独的通用序列效应相比，这种模式与矿化开始时出现的酸性表面中 糖链 施加的变化更加一致。 图 4K 到 N 然后将相同的比较折叠到整个界面级别。 在这些面板中，鸡 保留了最容易访问的 Ca²⁺相关 表面，鸽 将最大份额转移到受 糖链 影响的状态，鸭 趋向于可访问性较低的一侧，但没有在指标上与 鸽 或 apo 参考统一分离。 因此，鸡 保留了推测的最高 Ca²⁺ 捕获能力，以及与矿化开始时 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 的早期 Ca²⁺响应性 打开最兼容的状态。 鸭和鸽从不同的结构背景走向了低可达性一侧。 相同的排序与表型序列相匹配：鸡 将最密集的 乳突层 场与 由内向外加载 的最强局部响应结合起来，而 鸭 和 鸽 向较低响应侧收敛。 综合起来，图 4A 到 N 链接了共享 基质蛋白 上的 糖链 相关分离、糖链 几何结构、界面屏蔽和 Ca²⁺相关 可访问性。 (2, 5, 40) 此排序将 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 糖链 相关的 Ca²⁺可及性 置于 糖链调控的OVAL解折叠 和 成核位点暴露 的上游，为测试生成的 乳突层 组织是否影响本地 破壳力学 提供结构前提。

[043] 有限元 测试将共享的 卵齿 接口转换为显式的 由内向外加载 设计。 图5A显示了物种特异性背喙视图和用于加载的匹配的显微CT衍生的有限元壳碎片。 由于网格保留了物种特定的外壳几何形状，因此分析仍然锚定在形态学上识别的相同 乳突层 上下文中。 在 蛋壳 碎片上的多个偏移位置对冲击载荷进行采样，产生每个物种的独立接触力和接触剪切应力时间过程（图 5B 和 C）。 我们记录了原始峰值接触力 (F_max) 和峰值接触剪切应力 (τ_max)，以便可以在 乳突层 接触界面处将厚度驱动力效应与 局部应力响应 分开。 峰值 τ_max 用作 破壳相关 本地外壳响应的直接读数。 物种平均值±标准差跨采样位置计算（图S11A至F；蛋壳厚度：鸡 0.29毫米、鸭 0.35毫米和鸽 0.19毫米）。 （6、44、45）
[046] 图5。 局部 有限元 加载将 卵齿 接触几何结构连接到 破壳相关蛋壳力学。 (A) 从上到下，鸡、鸽 和 鸭 的背喙视图和匹配的 显微CT 壳碎片 有限元 模型。 每个模型都显示了锥形 卵齿 冲击器和重建壳体几何形状上的代表性局部应力场。 (B) 九个冲击位置的平均接触力时间进程，带有阴影 ±1σ 包络线，以及相应的峰值接触力 (F_max) 分布。 星形标记表示每个时间过程轨迹上的平均峰值。 (C) 相同九个位置的平均接触剪应力时间过程，带有阴影 ±1σ 包络线，以及相应的峰值剪应力 (τ_max) 分布。 星形标记表示每条迹线上的平均峰值。 箱线图点表示个体影响位置（每个物种 n = 9），条形图显示平均值±s.d.，图上方的 p 值是单向方差分析综合 p 值，不同的字母表示 p < 0.05 处的 Tukey HSD 分组。 模拟使用相同的载荷和材料设置，以便比较隔离依赖于几何形状的局部响应。
[048] 不同物种之间的峰值 F_max 差异显着 (p = 1.64 × 10^-13)。 鸡达到1.12±0.11N，鸭达到0.90±0.09N，鸽达到0.49±0.04N，并且通过Tukey HSD，所有成对差异均显着（图5B）。 相比之下，τ_max 解析了两级模式 (p = 6.64 × 10^-10)。 鸡达到551.60±108.80 MPa，显着高于鸭的404.00±39.60 MPa和鸽的393.00±35.20 MPa。 后两个物种彼此之间没有显着差异（p = 0.728；图 5C）。
[049] F_max 和 τ_max 之间的差异阐明了 鸭 结果。 其较高的原始接触力主要是由较大的外壳厚度驱动的（鸽 为 0.35 毫米，而 鸽 为 0.19 毫米）。 它并不表明单位面积的材料电阻优异。 相比之下，鸡 相对于其他两个物种，τ_max 增加了 36-40%，这表明 破壳相关 局部应力响应 的能力更强，与壳厚度无关。 这种高与低分组（高组中单独存在 鸡，低组中存在 鸭 和 鸽）与 Tukey HSD 恢复的 乳突层 密度分组相匹配（图 1D）。 因此，该机制保留了从 乳突层 组织和 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 可访问性中恢复的对比。
[050] 尽管不存在相同的高密度 乳突层 状态，但仅凭整个壳断裂力就可以使 鸭 在机械性能上优于 鸡，因为其壳厚度更大。 通过将注意力集中在 显微CT 派生的 乳突层 接口上的 局部应力响应，τ_max 消除了这种歧义。 它表明高密度 鸡 状态仍然不同，而 鸭 和 鸽 收敛于较低的局部响应水平。 该功能读数保留了前面部分中已经可见的相同不对称性，并将 糖基化 相关差异与三个模型物种中的局部 破壳力学 联系起来。
[051] 讨论
[052] 该数据集中的跨物种差异首先在 乳突层 上解决，而不是在批发 基质蛋白 营业额水平上解决。 在一个广泛共享的 蛋壳 矩阵工具包中，OVAL糖链状态 提供了连接 Ca²⁺相关 表面可及性、乳突层 组织和 破壳相关 局部力学的最清晰的分子轴。 （1、6、23）
[053] 三物种设计很重要，因为 蛋壳 性状是沿着连续的生态和发育梯度组织的，而不是通过一个二元标签组织的。 (12, 14) 筑巢环境遵循陆地到水生的轴，后代的状况跨越从早成到晚成的幼体的连续体。 这两个维度都不能通过简单的是或否划分来很好地表示。 (42, 43) 鸭 在该设计中提供了特别丰富的信息。 在此比较中，它保留了广泛的早熟发育条件和最大的壳厚度，但它转向中间的 OVAL糖链状态 和可达性概况，并且其 τ_max 结果与 鸽 收敛，而不是与 鸡 收敛。 因此，鸭 将外壳厚度缓冲与此处强调的 Ca²⁺可及性-乳突层-力学轴 分开。 比较样本故意分隔连续生态发展空间的区域，同时保持孵化界面本身的可比性。 (12, 41–43)
[054] 乳突层 成矿模式仍然是解释中的中心构造层。 (1, 17) 在此模型中，糖链 依赖性的酸性 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 表面暴露会在最早的基质-矿物质相互作用期间影响 Ca²⁺可及性 和 OVAL解折叠，从而改变 成核位点呈现、乳突层形成、成熟乳突层密度 和 局部破壳力学。 一旦建立了早期的方解石晶体单元，后来的 蛋壳 区域就会继承第一个矿化窗口中设置的间距逻辑。 因此，密集的 乳突层 场可以改变基质保留、矿物连续性、局部应力重新分布和成熟形态。 (1, 46, 47) 这一重点与早期的 蛋壳 研究一致，该研究将 乳突层 置于晶体成核和基体控制的交叉点。 目前的比较通过将该层链接到跨物种 糖链状态 读数而不是单独链接到外壳质量描述符来扩展该线。 (1, 17, 48, 49) 最近的家禽组学研究越来越多地将年龄、壳腺转录、细胞外囊泡货物和其他全壳质量性状与 蛋壳 表型联系起来。 然而，这些描述符通常比此处隔离的邻近材料层更广泛。 (18, 50–52) 因此，乳突层 组织不仅是另一个壳特征，而且是基质化学可以合理地影响后来的机械结果的最早的材料背景。 (1, 4) 这种结构位置就是为什么 乳突层 组织是第一个跨物种差异，它可以被解读为潜在的后果，而不仅仅是描述性的。 (1, 17)
[055] 在这里检查的分子层中，卵清蛋白（OVAL） N-糖链 架构最密切地跟踪了跨物种恢复的结构对比。 原形群更替、基因家族变化和 糖蛋白 网络分歧仍然很重要，但它们主要定义比较背景而不是最接近的解释。 OVAL糖链状态 的信息尤其丰富，因为它是跨物种共享的、化学上可解释的，并且位于已经与矿化有关的丰富的 基质蛋白 上。 (2, 23, 53) 早期工作已将 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 视为丰富的 蛋壳 糖蛋白 和矿化候选者。 先前的糖蛋白质组学研究还表明，蛋壳-基质蛋白 可以占据不同的 N-糖基化 状态。 因此，这里的进步不仅仅是检测到更多的 糖肽；它是直系同源解析的比较，其中 糖链状态 与表型最一致，以及这些分配如何进行结构和机械解释。 (2, 23) 早期的 鸡 研究为 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 建立了糖位点基础，并在 OC116 中鉴定了糖基化 Asn。 本数据集解析了结构建模中相应 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 直系同源序列的主要 糖链 类（鸡 N293；鸭 和 鸽 N97）。 相对于早期的位点检测研究，这种比较扩展了物种间的广度并扩展到 糖链 类解释，而不仅仅是延长了种内位点库存。 (21, 23, 28) 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 有用，不是因为它是独特的，而是因为它在物种之间保持可比性，同时在特定 糖链 类别水平上保留可解释的化学差异。 （21、23、28）
[056] 非 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 信号仍然很重要。 OC116和TRFE仍然是信息丰富的共享蛋白，而OC17仅在鸡中出现糖基化，因此可能代表一种更受谱系限制的矿化程序。 (5, 19, 20) 早期的 蛋壳 研究已经将功能重要性赋予了 OC17、OC116 和卵转铁蛋白相关基质成分，并且当前的数据集并未推翻该观点。 相反，这些蛋白质对生物背景的定义比对这里发现的主要跨物种鉴别器的定义更加一致。 (5, 19, 20) 特别是对于 OC116，早期的生化工作已经在 鸡 蛋壳 基质中建立了糖基化 Asn。 最近的鸟类古蛋白质组学工作表明，OC116 是序列变异较多的鸟类 蛋壳 蛋白之一。 它显示出显着的种内变异性，而不是作为一致稳定的物种标记。 这种更广泛的背景适合我们的比较：保留的 糖基化 保持了 OC116 的生物学相关性，但它不能恢复与 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 一样稳定的结构相关对比度。 (21, 31) 同样的一点也阐明了本研究与早期 蛋壳 糖蛋白组学的关系。 这些研究对于证明 蛋壳 基质蛋白 可以被糖基化并且位点水平库存可以通过实验获得至关重要。 我们的贡献是下一个比较步骤：共享的直系同源物、占主导地位的 糖链 类，以及这些类在跨物种表面呈现的结果。 (21, 23) 因此，尽管 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 提供了实验上最容易处理的入口点，但共享工具包仍然是多组件的。
[057] Re-Glyco和APBS分析为论证提供了结构桥梁。 在不同物种中，这产生了 糖链状态 梯度：紧凑的 鸡 糖链 保留了最容易接近的酸性表面，中性复合物/杂化 鸭 糖链 施加了中间约束，而扩展的唾液酸化 鸽 糖链 产生了最强的空间和静电屏蔽。 早期的体外和结构工作已经表明，卵清蛋白（OVAL） 构象和静电在矿化过程中很重要，但匹配的糖型解析表面整体尚未在鸟类物种之间进行比较。 (2, 40) 糖链状态 变化因此在这里被解析为物理上可解释的表面差异。 尽管这一结果并未建立直接因果关系，但它支持了一个有限的推论：同一 基质蛋白 上的不同 糖链状态 可以改变呈现给矿化环境的化学表面，从而可能导致此处观察到的结构差异。
[058] 机械比较的目标是孵化期间的 由内向外加载，而不是传统的外部压缩或全壳断裂。 这种选择至关重要：蛋壳 厚度会增加绝对失效载荷，而 τ_max 受厚度影响较小，并且更直接地报告通过 乳突层 接口的应力传递。 (6, 45, 54) Sun等 同样通过纵向和横向测量表明，蛋壳 的厚度在整个鸡蛋上有所不同，钝端周围的圆周区域局部最薄，这加强了将全局壳厚度缓冲与局部孵化界面力学分开的需要。 (55) 因此，分析询问内部 乳突层 界面是否保留已从基质化学和形态推断出的相同对比度。 (6, 45, 54, 56) 鸭 使这种分离变得明确：其较厚的壳增加了 F_max，但没有重现 鸡 中观察到的高 τ_max 状态。 这种区别将厚度缓冲和发育背景与此处强调的材料途径分开。 蛋壳 的厚度、体型、广泛的生殖生态和谱系历史都有助于背景结构。 (43, 57) 然而，仅厚度解释并不能解释 τ_max，并且扩散谱系分歧解释不能解释为什么相同的顺序在 糖链 类、静电可及性、乳突层 组织和 破壳相关 力学中重复出现。 (6, 44) 重复出现的是相同的对齐方式：糖链状态、Ca²⁺ 表面可访问性、乳突层 组织以及 由内向外加载 下的 τ_max。 生态学和系统发育定义了设计空间，而 基质蛋白 糖链状态 仍然是这里发现的最接近的化学可解释层。 (2, 23) 鸭 和 鸽 仍然至关重要，因为它们从不同方向约束 破壳相关 机械轴。 鸭 将更大的壳厚度与中等 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 可及性和低 τ_max 相结合，表明仅靠厚度并不能重建 鸡 局部响应状态。 鸽 在低 τ_max 下与 鸭 收敛，壳更薄，卵清蛋白（OVAL） 糖链 和不同的 乳突层 背景。 总之，这些对比使 鸡 成为将 糖链 相关的 基质行为 链接到 蛋壳 状态的有用参考，该状态将局部机械响应集中在孵化界面处。 在此框架中，OVAL糖链状态 是最直接可读的分子层，通过它该状态变得可机械解释。
[059] 第二个解释问题是 乳突层 在孵化后期和孵化过程中可能会被部分吸收。 这种可能性并不会消除当前比较的相关性，因为这里使用的量化描述符是 乳突层 密度和晶体单元组织。 即使最里面的部分材料被吸收，这些特征仍然嵌入在外壳中。 机械读数也遵循同样的考虑。 我们强调第二个特征峰而不是第一个，因为最早的力偏移更强烈地受初始形态相关接触的支配，而后来的峰更忠实地反映了整个壳壁的应力传递。
[060] 综上所述，比较收敛于该数据集中的 有利于破壳 局部 蛋壳 状态。 鸡结合了最密集的乳突层场、屏蔽最少的Ca²⁺相关 卵清蛋白（OVAL）表面以及由内向外加载之下最强的局部应力响应。 鸭 展示了为什么链不能减小到壳厚度：尽管它的壳比 鸽 更厚且 F_max 更高，但它的 τ_max 与 鸽 而不是 鸡 分组。 该模式支持这样的推论：在比较中，重复使用的 基质蛋白 上的化学特异性状态可以比单独的蛋白质组周转更直接地组织矿化表型。 (58–60)
[061] 相同的分析序列可能会扩展到鸟类 蛋壳 之外。 许多生物矿化系统依赖于有机基质，通过化学特定的界面状态而不是单独的本体成分来调节离子进入、表面暴露和矿物成核。 在更广泛的框架中，当前的工作流程提供了一种从糖蛋白质组状态转移到表面呈现，然后再转移到介观功能的方法。 其他矿化组织和仿生材料中也出现类似的水垢桥接问题。 同样的逻辑也可能为再生环境提供信息，其中 蛋壳 衍生的材料或膜蛋白正在探索用于组织工程和骨修复。 更广泛地说，该研究提供了一个模板，用于测试化学特定基质状态如何在生物和生物医学背景下组织生物矿物行为。 （58、59、61、62）
[062] 目前的范围仍然有限。 我们分析了主要糖型而不是完整的体内 糖链 整体。 我们还将每个物种在平均 蛋壳 的尺度上视为机械均匀，并依赖于 APBS 框架中不完全约束的子宫离子条件。 即时湿实验室跟进的一个实际限制是，使用当前的工具包，在单个框架内的特定 糖基化 位点上实现直系同源 基质蛋白 的物种匹配表达和预定义 糖链 结构的精确安装在实践中仍然很重要。 与此同时，生物矿化作为一个大型耦合反应系统运行，这可能会限制任何一种测定的直接单变量解释。 因此，下一个决定性测试是定义糖型矿化测定、在 鸡 中直接操作 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 糖基化、对相同 由内向外 机械对比度进行位点解析验证，以及提高因果分辨率的方法开发。 这些实验应该澄清 OVAL糖链状态 是否直接参与壳矿化，或者以异常保真度标记高 乳突层 密度 蛋壳 状态。
[063] 总之，本研究将 乳突层 组织、糖蛋白 状态、Ca²⁺ 表面可访问性和 局部破壳力学 跨三个鸟类 蛋壳 联系起来。 鸡定义了该轴的高乳突层密度端，具有密集的乳突层组织、紧凑的卵清蛋白（OVAL） 糖链、更大的Ca²⁺相关表面暴露以及乳突层接口处最强的局部响应。 鸭 占据了关键的中间位置：其较厚的外壳增加了绝对力，但没有重现相同的局部应力状态，将外壳厚度与 乳突层界面机制 分开。 随着可比较的糖型分配变得可用，相同的框架可以扩展到其他丰富的 蛋壳 基质蛋白。 在形态、糖蛋白组、结构和机械层中，OVAL糖链状态 仍然是此处回收的高 乳突层 密度 蛋壳 状态最一致的分子特征。
[064] 材料和方法
[065] 生物材料
[066] 从三个禽系中收集产蛋中期的受精蛋：茶花粉壳蛋鸡 7 个蛋、绍兴斑绿壳蛋 鸭s 蛋 7 个、白王蛋 鸽s 蛋 19 个。 Gallus gallus鸡蛋获自中国农业大学家禽资源保护场（中国北京）； Columba livia种蛋由中国农业大学兽医学院常宇教授提供； Anas platyrhynchos鸡蛋由金星鸭实业（中国北京）提供。 分析前，所有蛋均在种蛋保存条件下于 16°C 保存 7 天。
[067] 蛋壳 基质蛋白 提取
[068] 通过已建立的 EDTA 脱盐方案从 蛋壳 乳突层 (EML) 中提取 蛋壳 基质蛋白。 将鸡蛋用去离子水冲洗并放入无菌密封袋中。 每个物种保留一个卵用于 显微CT 分析；剩余的壳用于 基质蛋白 提取。 剩余的 6 个 鸡 蛋和 6 个 鸭 蛋被分为每个物种的 3 个两蛋提取单元，而剩余的 18 个 鸽 蛋被分为 3 个 6 蛋提取单元。 这些合并的提取单元用作鸟枪法蛋白质组学和完整 糖肽 分析的匹配生物样品。 对于原鸡和绿头鸭，在 EML 提取之前，用 15 mL 的 5% EDTA（0.13 mol/L，pH 7.6）补充 2-巯基乙醇（10 mmol/L）在 20°C 处理 30 分钟，并轻轻手动揉捏以分离 ECL，从而去除 蛋壳 角质层 (ECL)；随后用去离子水冲洗 蛋壳。 鸽 蛋壳s 中没有识别出离散的角质层，因此用蒸馏水冲洗 C. livia 壳并直接进入相同的提取工作流程。 然后，将来自所有三个物种的 EML 蛋白在相同的 EDTA-2-巯基乙醇条件下溶解，并将提取时间延长至 20°C 下的 12 小时。将所得悬浮液以 1,000 × g 离心 15 分钟；将沉淀重新悬浮并第二次离心，将合并的上清液储存在-80°C直至分析。 在相同的提取化学条件下并行处理物种，因此下游差异不太可能反映处理漂移。
[069] 显微CT 成像和 乳突层 形态测量
[070] 从靠近钝半中点的赤道区域切下每个物种的一个 蛋壳 碎片（4 毫米 × 4 毫米），并使用 Phoenix V|tome|x M 微焦点 CT 系统（德国文斯托夫 GE 传感和检测技术有限公司）进行扫描。 X射线源在85 kV管电压和160 μA电流下运行，没有束过滤器；所有标本的扫描设置保持不变，每个 蛋壳 总共收集了 1,800 个投影图像。 重建的图像体积被导出为 16 位无符号各向同性数据集，沿 x、y 和 z 轴（约 3.84 μm 体素大小）的采样距离约为 0.003836 mm。 扫描后，每个片段被均匀地细分为九个子片段，用于下游区域定量。 在 3D Slicer 中重建体积数据，并在自动选择后使用操作员引导的阈值校正，通过分割工作流程中的阈值模块对 蛋壳 区域进行分割。 同一操作员并排审查了所有三个物种；灰度阈值经过微调，以保留所有真实的壳体素，同时保留可见的孔隙开口。 采集噪声通过 5 × 5 × 5 中值滤波器进行抑制，然后进行最大岛隔离和 9 × 9 × 9 孔填充。 在每个子片段内，然后根据标签图计算三个形态参数。 分段壳模型首先被复制为单副本体积，并通过填充孔操作进行实体填充；从填充固体中减去原始壳模型产生了 乳突层 间隙层，并且出现在该间隙平面中的封闭空隙被定义为 乳突层 旋钮。 乳突层 密度计算为乳头计数除以分析单位面积。 同一分析单元中的总 蛋壳 体积直接从标签图中获得，平均柱单元体积定义为总壳体积除以乳头计数；然后将柱单元体积分数计算为平均柱单元体积除以相应分析单元的总 蛋壳 体积。 由于乳头发起的柱状单元在正常鸟类 蛋壳 微观结构中排列为重复且近似均匀的平面单元，因此这些参数被视为整个壳组织的局部平均代表。 每个物种的九个观察结果是来自一个扫描片段的不重叠的区域子样本，用于量化围绕该局部平均值的片段内空间变化，而不是代表九个独立的蛋。 相同的分割和后处理工作流程应用于所有扫描，以便物种对比反映形态而不是重建设置。 (1, 46)
[071] 蛋壳 基质蛋白s 的鸟枪法蛋白质组学
[072] 对于鸟枪法蛋白质组学，来自 CT 后样品组的 蛋壳 基质提取物被分析为每个物种的三个合并生物样品，每个 鸡 和 鸭 样品包含两个鸡蛋，每个 鸽 样品包含六个鸡蛋。 通过重悬于裂解缓冲液（1% SDS、1% 蛋白酶抑制剂混合物）中、冰上超声处理并在 4°C 下以 12,000 × g 离心 10 分钟进行澄清来提取蛋白质；使用 BCA 测定试剂盒测定蛋白质浓度。 用预冷的丙酮（5 倍体积，-20°C，2 小时）沉淀蛋白质，用丙酮洗涤两次，然后重新溶解在 200 mM TEAB 中。 用 5 mM 二硫苏糖醇还原二硫键（56°C，30 分钟），并用 11 mM 碘乙酰胺烷基化（室温，15 分钟，黑暗）。 使用测序级胰蛋白酶（酶：蛋白质比例 1:50）将它们消化过夜，并使用 Strata X SPE 柱脱盐。 LC-MS/MS 分析之前未进行离线 HPLC 分级。 对于蛋白质组采集，每次运行注射 20 μg 肽。
[073] 将脱盐的肽溶解在流动相 A（0.1% 甲酸水溶液）中，并在自制的 15-cm × 100-μm i.d. 柱上分离。 连接至 Vanquish Neo UPLC 系统 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) 的反相 C18 分析柱。 流动相 B 由 0.1% 甲酸的 80% 乙腈溶液组成，流速保持在 400 nl/min。 梯度程序为 4% B 0–0.5 分钟、4–8% B 0.5–0.6 分钟、8–22.5% B 0.6–13.6 分钟、22.5–35% B 13.6–20.5 分钟、35–55% B 20.5–20.9 分钟、55–99% B 20.5–20.9 分钟。 20.9–21.4 分钟，99% B 21.4–22.6 分钟。 UPLC 分离后，将肽引入运行电压为 1,900 V 的纳米电喷雾电离源，并在 Orbitrap Astral 质谱仪（Thermo Fisher Scientific）上进行分析。 全 MS 谱图在 Orbitrap 中以 240,000 分辨率在 380–980 m/z 范围内获得；在 Astral 分析仪中使用 HCD 碎片以 DIA 模式 (NCE=25%)、固定第一质量 150 m/z、AGC 目标 500% 和最大注射时间 3 ms 以 80,000 分辨率采集 MS/MS 碎片。 DIA 数据使用 DIA-NN v1.8 针对物种特异性参考蛋白质组进行处理 - G. gallus（43,711 个条目）、A. platyrhynchos（91,801 个条目）和 C. livia（17,309 个条目），所有数据均于 2024 年 8 月下载 - 与反向诱饵序列连接。 使用物种匹配的参考数据库可以降低肽分配偏向注释最佳的蛋白质组的风险。 胰蛋白酶/P 裂解被指定为最多有 1 个缺失裂解； N-末端甲硫氨酸切除和Cys的脲甲基化被设置为固定修饰。 蛋白质和肽FDR均控制在<1%。
[074] 完整的糖肽质谱仪
[075] 对于完整的 糖肽 分析，每个物种的三个相同的合并生物样本被继续进行，并且来自每个合并样本的 200 μg 肽消化物被用作富集的输入。 N-糖肽 通过亲水相互作用液相色谱 (HILIC) 从胰蛋白酶消化物中富集。 将肽消化物重新溶解在上样缓冲液（80% ACN、5% TFA）中，上样到 HILIC 柱上，用上样缓冲液洗涤 3 次，并用 0.1% TFA、50 mM 碳酸氢铵和 50% ACN 洗脱 糖肽 两次。 洗脱液用 C18 Zip-Tips 脱盐并真空干燥。 糖肽 级分在同一纳米 LC 平台上使用 34 分钟梯度（400 nl/min 时为 4–99% B）进行分离。 在 700–2,000 m/z 范围内以 240,000 分辨率获得全 MS 谱图； MS/MS 谱图以 80,000 分辨率采集，固定第一质量 120 m/z，循环时间 0.6 s，AGC 目标 100%，强度阈值 25,000 离子/s，最大进样时间 5 ms。 原始 DDA 数据使用 MSFragger v3.4 针对与上述相同的物种特异性参考蛋白质组进行处理，具有严格的胰蛋白酶切割（最多 2 个遗漏切割）、肽长度 7-50 个残基、固定 Cys 的脲甲基化、可变的 N 末端乙酰化和 Met 氧化以及默认的 MSFragger 糖基化 质量偏移。 蛋白质、肽和 PSM FDR 均控制在 < 1%。 使用牛津命名法将 N-糖链 结构类别分为六类：高甘露糖、寡甘露糖/截短、中性复合物/杂合体、岩藻糖基化复合物/杂合体、唾液酸化复合物/杂合体和其他。 每个蛋白质每个物种类别的丰度计算为总 糖链 位点信号强度的分数。 在位点水平识别后分配结构类别，以便可以在跨物种的共同蛋白质背景上比较共享的 糖链 使用情况。
[076] 比较蛋白质组分析和基因家族进化
[077] 使用 OrthoFinder（全部与全部 BlastP；E 值阈值 1 × 10⁻10；MCL 膨胀参数 2.0）将所有三个物种的蛋白质序列分配至正交群，为所有下游富集分析产生 3,250 个正交群背景集。 分歧时间取自已发布的时间树：G. gallus–A. platyrhynchos 83.37 Mya 和 G. gallus–C. livia 90.84 Mya。 基因家族的扩张和收缩率是通过 CAFE5 使用时间校准的系统发育来推断的。 使用 OrthoVenn3 网络平台 (https://OrthoVenn3.bioinfotoolkits.net) 在 3,250 个正交群背景下对成对共享、物种专有、扩展和收缩正交群集进行 GO 富集；调整后的 p<0.05 的项被认为是显着的。 （43、57、63）

[080] 综合proteiN-糖链丰度比较
[081] 对于图 3A 至 C，从 Protein_quant 和 Site_quant 表中分别加载每个物种的蛋白质水平和 糖链 位点定量表。 当存在 Number Comparable 字段时，排除值 < 2 的蛋白质条目和 糖链 位点条目，以便仅保留在三个合并生物样品中的至少两个中检测到的特征用于下游可比蛋白质分析。 计算每个登录的所有物种匹配强度列的平均蛋白质强度，并计算相应位点强度列中每个量化 糖基化 位点的平均 糖链 强度。 仅保留正强度条目。 然后通过蛋白质加入将 糖链 位点行内连接到蛋白质行，以便每个点代表一个量化的糖基化序列序列，并具有匹配的蛋白质丰度测量值。 蛋白质和 糖链 强度进行 log2 转换，并通过与两侧 p 值的 Spearman 等级相关来总结种内蛋白质 N-糖链 耦合。 卵清蛋白（OVAL）、OC116、TRFE 和 OC17 通过图 2E 中总结的严格直向同源分配突出显示，并且标签用相应的糖基化 Asn 位置进行注释。

[083] N-糖链结构整体建模
[084] 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 直系同源蛋白结构（AlphaFold2 预测模型）通过 GlycoShape 平台 (GlycoShape.org) 通过 UniProt 登录标识符进行访问。 通过单同位素质量匹配（公差 ±0.5 Da），使用每个残留物质量 203.0794 Da (HexNAc)、162.0528 Da (Hex)、291.0954 Da，将来自 IGP-MS 的实验检测到的 N-糖链 组合物映射到 GlycoShape 糖链 文库。 (NeuAc)、146.0579 Da (dHex) 和 132.0423 Da (Pen)，并进行 18.0106 Da 水校正；匹配的条目被检索为 GlyTouCan 加入标识符。 所有实验检测到的可与 GlycoShape 库匹配的糖型都被保留用于下游建模，而不是预过滤到主要子集。 然后使用 GlycoShape Re-Glyco 整体工具 (GlycoShape.org/ensemble) 生成完整的构象系综，该工具通过将它们与 Privateer 晶体学标准中的扭转角对齐并从 GlycoShape 分子动力学系综库中采样构象来恢复丢失的 糖链。 对于每个 卵清蛋白（OVAL） 直系同源物，糖链 建模仅限于当前数据集中确定的实验检测到的 糖肽 位点；然后，每个匹配的 糖链 通过 GlycoShape API 作为独立建模作业提交，并附加到测量的目标序列 - G. gallus N293 和 A. platyrhynchos / C. livia N97 - 集合大小为 50，随机种子 42 和 PDB 输出格式。 此过程产生了 50 个原鸡 糖蛋白 模型（1 个匹配的 糖链 类型）、150 个绿头鸭模型（3 个类型）和 700 个 C. livia 模型（14 个类型，包括从 GlycoShape 库中解析出的四种 NeuAc 位置异构体）。 对每个提交的 糖链 使用相同的随机种子和集合大小，保持跨物种的匹配采样深度。 使用 BioPython 从原子坐标计算每个模型的整体几何描述符：所有 糖链 重原子的 回转半径 (Rg)、糖链 链的端到端距离以及任何 糖链 重原子和蛋白质 Cα 原子之间的最小距离（最小 Cα 接触距离）。 对每个描述符进行每个结构的汇总统计（平均值±s.d.）和成对物种比较（Mann-Whitney U 检验，双尾）。 （40、64、65）
[085] 静电势计算
[086] 计算每个 Re-Glyco 整体模型的静电表面电势以及使用 APBS v3.4.1 删除 糖链 的匹配 apo 参考。 使用 CHARMM36 力场和 pH 7.4 下的 PROPKA 质子化，用 PDB2PQR 分配原子部分电荷和半径； 糖链 重原子部分电荷是根据已发布的 GLYCAM06 值分配的。 APBS 输入网格是从 PQR 边界框自动生成的，填充量为 10 Å，目标网格间距为 0.5 Å；聚焦网格设置为粗网格长度的 70%。 使用单离子边界条件、0.15 mol/L mon卵清蛋白（OVAL）ent 盐（阳离子和阴离子的离子半径分别为 2.0 和 1.8 Å）、蛋白质电介质 2.0、溶剂电介质 78.54、溶剂可及表面定义 smol、电荷离散化 spl0、溶剂探针半径 1.4 Å、样条窗口求解非线性 Poisson-Boltzmann 方程0.3 Å，表面密度 10.0，温度 298.15 K。 溶剂可及表面积通过 Shrake-Rupley 算法计算；表面残留物定义为相对 ASA ≥ 0.25。 Ca²⁺ 结合静电 热点 被定义为 APBS 电位 < -5 kT/e 的表面 Asp 或 Glu 残基。 报告的集合级指标包括 热点 计数 (N_hot)、每个 热点 平均 SASA、热点 总表面 Asp/Glu 分数以及中值表面静电势。 相同的静电阈值和表面定义规则应用于所有模型，以保持跨物种可比性。 (32, 33, 66)
[087] 有限元分析
[088] 用于下游 有限元 分析的感兴趣区域被定义为 显微CT 重建期间半径为 1 mm 的圆柱形体积。 从 显微CT 导出的表面模型首先导出为 STL 文件，并在 Geomagic Wrap 中进行逆向工程，通过顺序去噪（强度 2）进行 有限元 预处理、将三角形简化为约 300,000 个面、以 0.01 mm 进行网格重新网格化、迭代缺陷校正至零残余故障以及以最小公差进行有机参数表面拟合。 然后将生成的 蛋壳 表面模型导入 Ansys Workbench 2023 R1 中，并使用显式 LS-DYNA 模块进行求解（单位系统：mm/kg/N/s）。 为了隔离结构驱动的机械差异，不同物种之间分配了相同的 蛋壳 材料属性；参数值采用 Biology 10, 989 (2021; DOI: 10.3390/biology10100989) 中报告的鸟类 蛋壳 弹性研究，而不是针对每个物种单独重新估计。 在求解器关键字组中，蛋壳 使用 *MAT_PLASTIC_KINEMATIC 和 *SECTION_SOLID 进行建模，使用密度 2770 kg/m^3、杨氏模量 3.0 × 1010 Pa、泊松比 0.33、屈服强度 1.5 × 10⁷ Pa、切线模量 0 和失效时最大等效塑性应变0.05。 这种显式冲击设置遵循碰撞变形模拟的一般逻辑，但已重新调整为此处研究的本地 蛋壳 加载几何形状。 用于模拟蛋齿的冲击器是一个截锥体（底部半径 0.1 毫米；顶部半径 0.5 毫米；高度 0.5 毫米），分配了 IRON-ARMCO 显式材料库，并作为单独的实体零件进行网格划分。 冲击器和 蛋壳 之间的接触通过 *CONTACT_AUTOMATIC_SURFACE_TO_SURFACE 定义，摩擦系数为 0.2。 蛋壳网格单元尺寸为0.05毫米（G. gallus）、0.05毫米（A. platyrhynchos）和0.03毫米（C. livia），确保蛋壳横截面上至少有六个单元层；冲击器的网格为0.1毫米。 冲击器由沿加载轴施加的 50,000 毫米/秒的初始速度驱动，而碎片周边上设置的一个边界在所有平移和旋转自由度上完全固定。 分析运行时间为 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ s，时间步长安全系数为 0.7，启用侵蚀，最小时间步长为 1 × 10⁻⁸ s，并自动质量缩放；求解器输出包括间隔为 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ s 的 GLSTAT、SPCFORC、RCFORC、NCFORC、BNDOUT、NODOUT、MATSUM、ELOUT、JNTFORC 和 DEFORC，每 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ s 写入 D3PLOT 和 INTFOR。 对于位置载荷分析，冲击器在 9 个横向偏移处采样，这些偏移排列在 3 × 3 网格上，间距为 0.5 mm。 在这九个案例中，仅转换了撞击器坐标；材料定义、接触设置、边界条件、片段大小和所有其他解算器控制保持不变。 提取每个位置的峰值接触力 (F_max) 和峰值接触剪应力 (τ_max)。 九个偏移采样允许测量局部位置异质性，而无需改变物种之间的片段大小或加载几何形状。 （6、44、67）
[089] 统计分析
[090] 所有值均表示为平均值±标准差。所有统计检验均为双尾，p < 0.05 被认为具有统计显着性。 在参数种间分析之前，通过 Shapiro-Wilk 检验评估正态性，通过 Levene 检验评估方差齐性。 通过单向方差分析，然后进行 Tukey 真实显着性差异检验（Tukey HSD；α=0.05），比较物种之间的 乳突层 形态参数。 每个物种的这九个形态测量观察结果是来自一个扫描片段的不重叠的子片段，因此应解释为片段内区域重复，而不是九个独立的生物样本。 相同的假设检查支持使用 Tukey HSD 对 有限元 结果（F_max 和 τ_max）进行单向方差分析。 相比之下，糖链 集合几何描述符（Rg、端到端距离、最小 糖链-蛋白质接触距离）和 热点 派生的集合度量不满足物种之间的正态性和/或同方差性，因此使用双边 Mann-Whitney U 检验评估这些变量的成对物种对比。 通过单样本 Wilcoxon 符号秩检验与 apo 参考值的比较，评估了 糖基化 诱导的 C. livia 内 N_hot 的减少；对整个界面水平的总 Asp/Glu SASA 进行了描述性总结，因为结构水平值在 C. livia 糖型中保持不变；通过单样本 Wilcoxon 符号秩检验相对于 apo 参考值来评估中位表面静电势的变化。 没有应用多重测试校正，也没有删除任何异常值。 所有统计分析均使用 scipy.stats 和 statsmodels 在 Python 中进行。
