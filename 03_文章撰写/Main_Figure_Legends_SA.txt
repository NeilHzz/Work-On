Fig. 1. Comparative framework and mammillary-layer morphology of the three focal avian species.
(A) AVONET-based comparison space used to select Gallus gallus, Anas platyrhynchos, and Columba livia as the focal species for cross-species comparison. (B) Phylogenetic relationship among the three focal species. (C) Representative micro-CT views of the mammillary layer, showing rounded mammillae in chicken, more ridged and angular mammillae in duck, and discrete triangular-conical mammillae in pigeon. (D) Quantification of mammillary knob density and the fraction of the eggshell occupied by crystal units extending from individual mammillary knobs. Chicken showed the highest mammillary knob density, pigeon showed the highest crystal-unit proportion, and duck was intermediate.

Fig. 2. Orthogroup structure and lineage-specific functional divergence of eggshell matrix proteomes.
(A) Venn diagram summarizing shared, pairwise-shared, and species-specific orthogroups in the three eggshell matrix proteomes. (B) Phylogenetic tree reconstructed from eggshell matrix protein repertoires. (C to E) Gene Ontology enrichment for the three pairwise-shared orthogroup sets. (F) Gene Ontology enrichment for species-specific orthogroups, highlighting protein N-linked glycosylation in the chicken-specific set and immune or developmental functions in the duck- and pigeon-specific sets. (G) Gene-family expansion and contraction across the three species. (H) Functional enrichment of the expanded and contracted gene-family sets. Together, these analyses define the evolutionary background against which the ordered eggshell phenotype was interpreted.

Fig. 3. Cross-species glycoprotein conservation and ortholog screening.
(A) Circular network linking orthologous glycoproteins to seven glycan classes across the three species, organized into a three-species conserved core together with pairwise-shared and lineage-restricted sectors. (B) BlastP-based ortholog comparison used to refine shared glycoprotein candidates for downstream analysis. This screen distinguished lineage-restricted targets from high-confidence shared anchors and prioritized OVAL for structural interpretation.

Fig. 4. Proteotype coevolution and glycosylation profiling identify OVAL as the leading shared candidate.
(A to C) Proteotype coevolution plots comparing protein abundance and glycan abundance within each species. Protein-glycan coupling was weak in chicken and stronger in duck and pigeon, indicating species-specific relationships between bulk protein abundance and glycan output. (D to G) Glycosylation profiling of four focal proteins, showing species-dependent glycan-class usage. OVAL displayed the clearest ordered shift, from high-mannose glycans in chicken toward more extended complex and sialylated states in duck and pigeon. (H to J) Pairwise two-dimensional glycan-protein enrichment analyses for the three species pairs. Together, these analyses identified OVAL as the shared target whose glycosylation state changed most consistently across species.

Fig. 5. Re-Glyco structural ensembles define an OVAL accessibility gradient across species.
(A) Per-residue surface electrostatic potential profiles for glycosylated and deglycosylated OVAL models from the three species. (B) Ca2+ hotspot residue counts. (C) Carboxylate surface accessibility. (D) Surface-potential distribution. (E to H) Ensemble geometric descriptors of the rebuilt glycans, including glycan radius of gyration, end-to-end distance, glycan-protein distance, and minimum distance to Ca2+-relevant surface features. (I) Interface shielding by glycan. (J) Mean SASA of hotspot residues. (K) Hotspot fraction among candidate acidic residues. (L) Net accessible Ca2+ hotspots after glycan shielding. (M) Partition of Ca2+ hotspot accessibility into net accessible and glycan-shielded components. (N) Partition of hotspot-residue SASA into net accessible and glycan-shielded components. Across these metrics, chicken retained the most exposed Ca2+-relevant surface, pigeon showed the strongest glycan-mediated shielding, and duck remained intermediate.

Fig. 6. Mechanical simulation links mammillary architecture to a hatching-relevant resistance phenotype.
(A) Finite-element simulation of inside-out loading on micro-CT-derived eggshell fragments. Upper panels show contact-force (F) fields and lower panels show shear-stress (tau) fields generated by a conical indenter applied to the inner eggshell surface. (B) Cross-species comparison of peak contact force (Fmax; left) and peak shear stress (tau_max; right). Fmax preserved a three-level hierarchy, whereas tau_max separated chicken from the lower-resistance duck and pigeon group, indicating that local resistance at the mammillary interface more closely tracked the structural ordering inferred from morphology and OVAL accessibility.
