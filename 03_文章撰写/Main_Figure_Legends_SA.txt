Fig. 1. Comparative framework and mammillary-layer morphology of the three focal avian species.
(A) AVONET-based comparison space used to select Gallus gallus, Anas platyrhynchos, and Columba livia as the focal species for cross-species comparison. (B) Phylogenetic relationship among the three focal species. (C) Representative micro-CT views of the mammillary layer, showing rounded mammillae in chicken, more ridged and angular mammillae in duck, and discrete triangular-conical mammillae in pigeon. (D) Quantification of mammillary knob density and the fraction of the eggshell occupied by crystal units extending from individual mammillary knobs. Chicken showed the highest mammillary knob density, pigeon showed the highest crystal-unit proportion, and duck was intermediate.

Fig. 2. Cross-species glycoprotein network architecture.
Circular network linking orthologous glycoproteins to seven glycan classes across the three species, organized into a three-species conserved core together with pairwise-shared and lineage-restricted sectors.

Fig. 3. Ortholog screening and abundance-decoupled glycoprotein prioritization.
(A) BlastP-based ortholog comparison used to refine shared glycoprotein candidates for downstream analysis. This screen distinguished lineage-restricted targets from high-confidence shared anchors and prioritized OVAL for structural interpretation. (B to D) Proteotype coevolution plots comparing protein abundance and glycan abundance within each species. (E to G) Pairwise two-dimensional glycan-protein enrichment analyses for the three species pairs. Together, these analyses identified OVAL as the shared target whose glycosylation state changed most consistently across species.

Fig. 4. Re-Glyco and APBS analyses define an OVAL accessibility gradient across species.
(A) Ca2+ hotspot residue counts. (B) Carboxylate surface accessibility. (C) Surface-potential distribution. (D to G) Ensemble geometric descriptors of the rebuilt glycans, including glycan radius of gyration, end-to-end distance, glycan-protein distance, and minimum distance to Ca2+-relevant surface features. (H) Interface shielding by glycan. (I) Mean SASA of hotspot residues. (J) Hotspot fraction among candidate acidic residues. (K) Net accessible Ca2+ hotspots after glycan shielding. (L) Partition of Ca2+ hotspot accessibility into net accessible and glycan-shielded components. (M) Partition of hotspot-residue SASA into net accessible and glycan-shielded components. Across these metrics, chicken retained the most exposed Ca2+-relevant surface, pigeon showed the strongest glycan-mediated shielding, and duck remained intermediate.

Fig. 5. Hatching-relevant loading design and finite-element simulation framework.
(A) Hand-drawn side and top views of the egg tooth and beak geometry in the three species. (B) Schematic illustration of egg-tooth impact against eggshell microstructure during hatching. (C) Finite-element simulation setup showing the three eggshell models and the corresponding egg-tooth impactor.

Fig. 6. Hatching-relevant force and shear-stress readouts across species.
(A) Time-course curves of contact force (F) together with bar-plot comparison of peak contact force (Fmax) across the three species. (B) Time-course curves of contact shear stress (tau) together with bar-plot comparison of peak shear stress (tau_max) across the three species. Fmax preserved a three-level hierarchy, whereas tau_max separated chicken from the lower-tau_max duck and pigeon group, indicating that the local contact-stress readout at the mammillary interface more closely tracked the structural contrast inferred from morphology and OVAL accessibility.
